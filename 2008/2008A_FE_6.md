### Explanation
An $m$-substring must be a consecutive sequence of characters. There are $k - m + 1$ possible starting positions in a string of length $k$, meaning there are $k - m + 1$ total $m$-substrings.
An $n$-subsequence does not need to be consecutive, so we are choosing $n$ characters out of $k$ characters without regard to order but keeping their relative positions. The number of ways to choose $n$ indices from $k$ is given by the combination formula ${k \choose n} = \frac{k!}{n!(k-n)!}$.
Therefore, choice A is the correct answer.
