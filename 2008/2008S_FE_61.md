# 2008S_FE_61

You are building a new database for a company with 10 departments. Each department contains multiple employees. In addition, each employee might work for several departments. How should you logically model the relationship between the department entity and the employee entity?

a) Create a mandatory one-to-many relationship between department and employee.
b) Create a new entry, create a one-to-many relationship from the employee to the new entry, and create a one-to-many relationship from the department entry to the new entry.
c) Create a new entry, create a one-to-many relationship from the new entry to the employee entry, and then create a one-to-many relationship from the entry to the department entry.
d) Create an optional one-to-many relationship between department and employee.
?
b) Create a new entry, create a one-to-many relationship from the employee to the new entry, and create a one-to-many relationship from the department entry to the new entry.

### Explanation
### Explanation
The question asks about resolving a many-to-many relationship between a department and an employee (an employee can work for several departments, and a department has multiple employees).

In relational databases, a many-to-many relationship is resolved by creating an associative entity (or intersection table). This new entity will have a many-to-one relationship to both original entities. From the perspective of the original entities, there is a one-to-many relationship from the original entity to the new associative entity.

Thus, one employee maps to many records in the new entity, and one department maps to many records in the new entity. This matches choice (b).
