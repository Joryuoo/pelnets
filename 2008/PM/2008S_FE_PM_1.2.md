The risk level of an activity causing a project delay depends on two primary factors: its position on the **critical path** and its **standard deviation**. 
*   **Standard Deviation** indicates the level of uncertainty or potential variance in the activity's duration. Higher standard deviation means the activity is less predictable.
*   If an activity is on the **critical path**, any increase in its duration directly delays the project. An activity with high variance that isn't on the critical path might just consume its slack time without affecting the final project deadline.

Let's evaluate the options:
*   **Hardware Installation (Activity 8):** Has a high standard deviation (1.41), but it is on the non-critical path (Path 2) which has 6 days of slack (45 - 39 = 6). A slight delay here won't delay the whole project.
*   **Implementation (Activity 10):** Standard deviation of 1.00.
*   **Program Development (Activity 5):** Has the highest standard deviation (1.41) alongside Hardware Installation, AND it is on the critical path (Path 1). Therefore, it has the highest risk of delaying the project.
*   **Requirement Study (Activity 1):** Standard deviation is 0.82.

Therefore, **Program Development** presents the highest risk to finishing the project as per the plan.
