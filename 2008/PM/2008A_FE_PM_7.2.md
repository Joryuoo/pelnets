### Explanation
- The statement `SuperClass obj = new SubClass();` creates an object of `SubClass` but assigns it to a reference of type `SuperClass`.
- `System.out.print(obj.greeting);`: In Java, instance variables are **not polymorphic**. They are resolved at compile-time based on the **reference type** (which is `SuperClass`). Therefore, it accesses `SuperClass`'s `greeting` variable, printing **Hello**.
- `obj.displayName();`: Instance methods **are polymorphic** (overridden). Method calls are resolved at runtime based on the **actual object type** (which is `SubClass`). Thus, the overridden `displayName()` method in `SubClass` is executed, printing ** SubClass!**.
- Therefore, the complete output is **Hello SubClass!**.
