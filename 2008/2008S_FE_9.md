# 2008S_FE_9

There are five different sizes of white balls and five different sizes of green balls.
When these ten balls are laid out in a line so that adjacent balls can have different colors, how many arrangements of the balls can be made?

a) 7,200
b) 14,400
c) 28,800
d) 57,600
?
c) 28,800

### Explanation
### Explanation
To ensure that adjacent balls always have different colors, the balls must strictly alternate in color. Since there are equal numbers of white (W) and green (G) balls, there are exactly two valid color patterns:
1. `W - G - W - G - W - G - W - G - W - G`
2. `G - W - G - W - G - W - G - W - G - W`

For each pattern:
- There are $5!$ (5 factorial) ways to arrange the 5 distinct sizes of white balls into the W slots.
- There are $5!$ ways to arrange the 5 distinct sizes of green balls into the G slots.

$5! = 5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 120$.
So, the number of arrangements for one specific pattern is $120 \times 120 = 14,400$.

Since there are 2 valid color patterns, the total number of arrangements is:
$14,400 \times 2 = 28,800$.
