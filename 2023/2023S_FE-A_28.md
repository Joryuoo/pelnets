# 2023S_FE-A_28

![[2023S_FE-A_Q28_full.png]]
?
b) An employee can belong to multiple departments at the same time.

### Explanation
The provided image shows a UML conceptual data model establishing an association between `Department` and `Employee`. 

Let's break down the diagram:
- The association name is **"Belongs to"** with a reading direction indicator (`◀`) pointing towards `Department`. This means we read it as: **"Employee Belongs to Department"**.
- The multiplicity on the `Department` side is `1..*` (one or more).
- The multiplicity on the `Employee` side is `0..*` (zero or more).

Reading the relationships:
1. **Employee $\rightarrow$ Department:** An Employee belongs to **`1..*`** Departments. This means every employee must belong to at least one department, and *can belong to multiple departments at the same time*.
2. **Department $\rightarrow$ Employee:** A Department has **`0..*`** Employees belonging to it. This means a department can have zero employees, or many employees.

Evaluating the choices based on our analysis:
* a) *A department cannot exist without any employees.* $\rightarrow$ **False.** The multiplicity is `0..*`, meaning 0 employees is allowed.
* b) *An employee can belong to multiple departments at the same time.* $\rightarrow$ **True.** The multiplicity `1..*` allows an employee to belong to more than one department.
* c) *An employee does not have to belong to a department.* $\rightarrow$ **False.** The minimum multiplicity is `1`, so they must belong to at least one.
* d) *The total number of employees and the total number of departments match.* $\rightarrow$ **False.** The multiplicities do not enforce a 1:1 total count ratio.
