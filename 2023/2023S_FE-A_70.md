### Explanation

To find the bottleneck or the process lacking in capability, we must calculate the total monthly production capacity for each process and see if it can meet the required production volume of **1,000 units per month**.

**Total Required Hours per Month for 1,000 units:**
$\text{Required Hours} = (\text{Manufacturing time per unit}) \times 1,000$

**Total Available Hours per Month for the Process:**
$\text{Available Hours} = (\text{Monthly production capacity per machine}) \times (\text{Number of machines})$

Let's calculate this for all processes:

| Process | Required Hours (for 1,000 units) | Available Capacity (Hours) | Difference (Capacity - Required) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | $0.4 \times 1,000 = 400$ | $150 \times 3 = 450$ | $450 - 400 = +50$ | Sufficient |
| B | $0.3 \times 1,000 = 300$ | $160 \times 2 = 320$ | $320 - 300 = +20$ | Sufficient |
| **C** | **$0.7 \times 1,000 = 700$** | **$170 \times 4 = 680$** | **$680 - 700 = -20$** | **Lacking (Bottleneck)** |
| D | $1.2 \times 1,000 = 1,200$ | $180 \times 7 = 1,260$ | $1,260 - 1,200 = +60$ | Sufficient |

Since Process C only has 680 available hours but requires 700 hours to produce 1,000 units, **Process C** is lacking in capability.
