### Explanation
**
Short-cut evaluation skips the second part of a logical condition if the outcome is already determined.
- For **Test case 1** (`x=11, a=9, b=19, c=10, d=10`):
  - `a < 10` is `9 < 10` (True). Since it's an `OR` condition, the whole condition is True. The right side `b < 20` is **not evaluated**.
  - `c > 10` is `10 > 10` (False). Since it's an `AND` condition, the whole condition is False. The right side `d > 10` is **not evaluated**.
  - Thus, `b < 20` and `d > 10` are not evaluated (Choice **c**).
- For **Test case 2** (`x=11, a=10, b=20, c=11, d=11`):
  - `a < 10` is `10 < 10` (False). It must evaluate `b < 20`.
  - `c > 10` is `11 > 10` (True). It must evaluate `d > 10`.
  - Thus, **all single conditions are evaluated** (Choice **g**).
