### Explanation

#### Subquestion 1: E-R Diagram Multiplicities
1. **Relationship A (Student to Registration):**
   - Each student must register their thesis topic. While they can work in groups, the `Registration` table shows that each registration entry is tied to a single `StudentID`. 
   - A student might not have registered yet, meaning a student has **zero or one** registration (`-o+`). 
   - A registration row always belongs to **exactly one** student (`-||`).
   - Thus, the relationship from Student to Registration is `||-------o+` (Option a).

2. **Relationship B (Instructor to Registration):**
   - Each instructor can instruct at most 5 students, meaning an instructor can have **zero or many** registrations (`-o<`). 
   - Each registration entry has **exactly one** `InstructorID` (`-||`).
   - Thus, the relationship from Instructor to Registration is `||-------o<` (Option b).

#### Subquestion 2: Finding Unregistered Students
The goal is to find students who are graduating in 2023 but have *not* registered a topic.
To achieve this, we need to list all students and see if they have a matching record in the `Registration` table.
- **Blank C:** We must use a `LEFT OUTER JOIN` (Option b) from `Student` to `Registration`. This keeps all student records and appends `NULL` for the columns of the `Registration` table if a student hasn't registered.
- **Blank D:** To filter out students who *have* registered, we check if the registration record is missing by ensuring the primary key of the registration table is null: `r.ID IS NULL` (Option b).

#### Subquestion 3: Executing SQL3
The query determines the students receiving the excellent thesis award. It does this by finding the students who achieved the maximum grade within their respective faculty.

**Step 1: Understand the `Result` Table**
| StudentID | StudentName | FacultyID | Grade |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| S2101 | Sarah | FIT | 85 |
| S2102 | Karina | FIT | 90 |
| S2103 | Mike | FIT | 90 |
| S2104 | Selena | FEE | 85 |
| S2105 | Peter | FEE | 80 |

**Step 2: Subquery `mg` (Max Grade per Faculty)**
```sql
SELECT FacultyID, MAX(Grade) AS MaxGrade FROM Result GROUP BY Result.FacultyID
```
| FacultyID | MaxGrade |
| :--- | :--- |
| FIT | 90 |
| FEE | 85 |

**Step 3: INNER JOIN `Result` with `mg`**
The main subquery joins `Result r` with the max grades `mg` on `FacultyID` and matches the grades:
- `FIT` max grade is 90. Karina (S2102) and Mike (S2103) have 90. Both are selected.
- `FEE` max grade is 85. Selena (S2104) has 85. She is selected.

Therefore, **three students** (S2102, S2103, S2104) receive the award.
