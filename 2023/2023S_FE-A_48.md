### Explanation
The **error embedding method** (also known as fault seeding or error seeding) is a technique used to estimate the number of latent (hidden) errors remaining in a software program.

Here are the variables:
*   $S$: Number of deliberately embedded (seeded) errors.
*   $m$: Number of discovered errors from the embedded errors.
*   $T$: Number of latent (unknown) errors originally in the software before testing.
*   $n$: Total number of discovered errors (both embedded and latent).
*   $n - m$: Number of discovered errors from the originally latent errors.

The fundamental assumption of fault seeding is that the ratio of discovered seeded errors to total seeded errors is equal to the ratio of discovered latent errors to total latent errors. 

Mathematically, this proportion is expressed as:
$$ \frac{\text{Discovered Embedded Errors}}{\text{Total Embedded Errors}} = \frac{\text{Discovered Latent Errors}}{\text{Total Latent Errors}} $$

Substituting the variables:
$$ \frac{m}{S} = \frac{n - m}{T} $$

This matches option a). You can then solve for $T$ to estimate the initial latent errors:
$$ T = \frac{S(n - m)}{m} $$
