### Explanation
**Subquestion 3 (Distinctive Graduate Award Calculation)**

**Option d is correct:** "Any student whose ECP is equal to ECPMin can still qualify for the Distinctive Graduate Award."
According to the formula: `ECScore = 100 × ( (ECP − ECPMin) / (ECPMax − ECPMin) )`
If a student's `ECP = ECPMin`, their `ECScore` will be `0`.
Their `OPS` will then be calculated as: `OPS = 0.85 × average academic score + 0.15 × 0`.
As long as the student meets all other prerequisites (Conduct >= Good, No score < 85) and their `average academic score` is high enough to make their `OPS >= 95` (meaning their average score must be roughly 111.76 or higher, assuming scores can exceed 100), they can still qualify for the Distinctive Graduate Award.

* **Option a is incorrect** because the criteria only requires "at least Good". There is no extra weight given to "Excellent" over "Good" in the OPS calculation.
* **Option b is incorrect** because even with a higher ECP and a "Good" conduct rating, a student might have a single subject score lower than 85, which would automatically disqualify them.
* **Option c is incorrect** because having an average academic score of 85 or higher is not enough to guarantee an `OPS >= 95`. For example, an average score of 85 even with a perfect `ECScore` of 100 would result in an `OPS` of `(0.85 × 85) + (0.15 × 100) = 87.25`, which does not qualify.
