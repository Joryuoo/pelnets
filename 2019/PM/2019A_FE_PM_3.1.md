### Explanation

To find customers who purchased "Fruit" products 30 times or more, totaling $300.00 or more between April 1 and June 30, 2019, we must correctly structure the SQL query to filter, group, and check aggregate conditions.

1. **Filtering Individual Records (WHERE clause)**:
   We must first restrict the data to the specified date range and product category before aggregating:
   `AND Bill.Date >= '2019-04-01' AND Bill.Date <= '2019-06-30'`
   `AND Product.Category = 'Fruit'`

2. **Grouping by Customer (GROUP BY clause)**:
   We aggregate the remaining records by customer:
   `GROUP BY Bill.CustomerID`

3. **Applying Aggregate Conditions (HAVING clause)**:
   We then apply conditions on the aggregated groups. The condition is that they purchased 30 times or more (`COUNT(*) >= 30`) and the total amount is $300.00 or higher (`SUM(Product.UnitPrice * BillDetail.Quantity) >= 300.00`).
   `HAVING SUM(Product.UnitPrice * BillDetail.Quantity) >= 300.00 AND COUNT(*) >= 30`

- **Option a** is incorrect because `Product.UnitPrice * BillDetail.Quantity >= 300.00` is in the `WHERE` clause, which means it would check if *each individual* purchase was >= 300.00, rather than the total sum per customer.
- **Option c** is incorrect because `Product.Category = 'Fruit'` is in the `HAVING` clause, but it is not an aggregated column nor is it in the `GROUP BY` clause.
- **Option d** is incorrect because filtering by `Bill.Date` and `Product.Category` should occur in the `WHERE` clause before grouping.

Therefore, **b** is the correct answer.
