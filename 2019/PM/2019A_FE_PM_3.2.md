### Explanation

**Blank B:**
Recording `SoldUnitPrice` in the `BillDetail` table preserves the historical price at which the item was actually sold. If the `Product` table's `UnitPrice` changes, it won't affect past records. Thus, the shop can obtain the transition of the sold unit price of a product that is purchased over time. **(b)**

**Blanks C, D, and E:**
The goal is to calculate the total bill amount by `Category` and customer `Type` for the 3rd quarter.
The query uses an inner subquery `BILL3Q` to join the tables and filter by date.
- **Blank D:** In the inner query, we need to extract the fields required to calculate the total bill amount. Since the exact historical unit price is now stored in `BillDetail.SoldUnitPrice`, we need both that and the quantity. The inner query does not group, so it simply selects `BillDetail.SoldUnitPrice, BillDetail.Quantity`. **(b)**
- **Blank C:** The outer query aggregates the results. To calculate the total bill amount, it must multiply the sold unit price by the quantity for each record and sum them up. Therefore, `SUM(SoldUnitPrice * Quantity) AS TotalBillAmount` is used. **(e)**
- **Blank E:** The outer query needs to group the data by customer type and product category as specified ("by category ... for each type"). So the `GROUP BY` clause must be `Type, Category`. **(g)**
