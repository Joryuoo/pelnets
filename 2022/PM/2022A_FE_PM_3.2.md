### Explanation
*
The query increases the salary of employees meeting a specific condition.
* **Blank D:** The statement modifies existing rows in the `Employee` table. The correct SQL command for modifying data is `UPDATE`. Thus, the statement should begin with `UPDATE Employee`. (Correct Answer: **e**)
* **Blank E:** The inner subquery joins `Order O` and `OrderDetail D`. While the query provided oddly uses the `ON` clause for the correlation condition (`ON O.EmployeeID = Employee.EmployeeID`), the actual tables `Order` and `OrderDetail` still need a join condition. This join condition must be placed in the `WHERE` clause. The tables share the `OrderID` column, so the condition is `O.OrderID = D.OrderID`. (Correct Answer: **d**)
* **Blank F:** The subquery returns `O.EmployeeID`. To filter employees based on the total amount of their orders, we must group the results by the employee and use the `HAVING` clause for the aggregate function `SUM()`. Therefore, `GROUP BY O.EmployeeID HAVING SUM(D.Amount) > 10000` is required. The `WHERE` clause cannot contain aggregate functions. (Correct Answer: **c**)
