### Explanation
**Blank G:**
To sort in descending order using Method 2, we must map the ascending positions to descending positions. Since there are 10 elements, valid output indices are 0 through 9. A score that would have been placed at position `n` in an ascending array must now be placed at the mirrored position from the end, which is `9 - n`. Thus, **G** is `9 - n`.

**Blank H:**
- **Method 1 Stability:** Method 1 changes Step 2 to iterate backwards (`i` from 10 down to 0). Higher scores are assigned lower starting indices. In Step 3, students are processed from left to right (0 to 9). The first student encountered with a score $S$ is placed at `Index[S]`, and `Index[S]` is incremented. The next student with score $S$ will be placed at `Index[S] + 1` (to the right). Their relative order is preserved, so **Method 1 is stable**.
- **Method 2 Stability:** Method 2 keeps Step 2 ascending. In Step 3, the first student with score $S$ gets index $n$, and is placed at $9 - n$. `Index[S]` is incremented to $n+1$. The next student with score $S$ gets index $n+1$, and is placed at $9 - (n+1) = 8 - n$. Since $8 - n$ is *before* $9 - n$, the second student is placed to the left of the first student, reversing their original order. Thus, **Method 2 is unstable**.

| Method | Loop Direction (Step 2) | Array Output Mapping (Step 3) | Relative Order | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Method 1** | Descending (`i=10` to `0`) | Ascending (`0` to `9`) | Maintains Left-to-Right | **Stable** |
| **Method 2** | Ascending (`i=0` to `10`) | Mirrored (`9-n`) | Reverses Left-to-Right | **Unstable** |

Therefore, **H** is method 1 is stable and method 2 is unstable.
