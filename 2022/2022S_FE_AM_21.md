### Deriving XNOR with NOR Gates

Let's evaluate the logic equation for circuit **d)** step by step. A NOR gate outputs the inverse of the logical OR of its inputs: $Y = \overline{A + B}$.

1. **First Stage Gate:**
   The inputs are $A$ and $B$.
   $$ C = \overline{A + B} $$
   *(Note: This is equivalent to $\overline{A} \cdot \overline{B}$ by De Morgan's Laws)*

2. **Second Stage Gates:**
   One gate takes inputs $A$ and $C$:
   $$ D = \overline{A + C} = \overline{A + (\overline{A} \cdot \overline{B})} $$
   Using the distributive law $X + \overline{X}Y = X + Y$:
   $$ D = \overline{A + \overline{B}} = \overline{A} \cdot B $$

   The other gate takes inputs $B$ and $C$:
   $$ E = \overline{B + C} = \overline{B + (\overline{A} \cdot \overline{B})} $$
   $$ E = \overline{B + \overline{A}} = A \cdot \overline{B} $$

3. **Final Stage Gate:**
   Takes inputs $D$ and $E$:
   $$ Y = \overline{D + E} $$
   Substitute $D$ and $E$:
   $$ Y = \overline{(\overline{A} \cdot B) + (A \cdot \overline{B})} $$

The expression $(\overline{A} \cdot B) + (A \cdot \overline{B})$ is the definition of an **Exclusive-OR (XOR)** gate ($A \oplus B$). Therefore, negating it gives us the **Exclusive-NOR (XNOR)** gate.

$$ Y = \overline{A \oplus B} = A \odot B $$



This confirms that the circuit in option **d** functions as an Exclusive-NOR (XNOR) gate.
