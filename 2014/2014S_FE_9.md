### Explanation
A binary search tree (BST) must uphold the property that for every node, all values in its left subtree are strictly less than its value, and all values in its right subtree are strictly greater than its value.

Looking at the structures:
- **Choice a)** satisfies the BST property. The root is 15. The left subtree has nodes 6, 2, 12, 7 (all $< 15$). The right subtree has nodes 18, 24 (all $> 15$). Within each subtree, the rule holds true recursively.
- The other choices fail the property. For example, they may place a larger value in a left subtree, or a smaller value in a right subtree (e.g. violating the ancestor bounds).
