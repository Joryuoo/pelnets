### Explanation
Let $S_1$ be the lower limit sensor and $S_2$ be the upper limit sensor. 
* Signal `0` means the water level is lower than the specified limit. 
* Signal `1` means the water level is at or above the specified limit.
When the water level is below the lower limit, both sensors output 0 ($00$). The faucet must be ON to fill the tank.
When the water level reaches the upper limit, both sensors output 1 ($11$). The faucet must be OFF to stop filling.
When the water is between the limits, $S_1 = 1$ and $S_2 = 0$ ($10$). The state of the faucet depends on whether the tank is currently being filled or drained:
- If it was filling (faucet ON) and reaches $10$, it should keep filling until $11$.
- If it was draining (faucet OFF) and reaches $10$, it should stay OFF until it drops below $00$.
Therefore, from the "Faucet ON" state, input `10` loops back to "Faucet ON", and input `11` transitions to "Faucet OFF". From "Faucet OFF", input `10` loops back to "Faucet OFF", and input `00` transitions to "Faucet ON". Input `01` is physically impossible (upper limit reached but lower limit not reached). 
This exactly matches the state transition diagram in choice a).
