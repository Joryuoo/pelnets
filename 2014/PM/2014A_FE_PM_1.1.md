### Explanation

**Blank A:**
According to Table 1, when $P=1, C=1, J=0,$ and $K=1$, the J-K flip-flop is in the **Reset** state. The rules for "Reset" dictate that the value of $Q$ becomes $0$ and $Q'$ (which is always the inverse of $Q$) becomes $1$. Therefore, at the subsequent clock fall, $Q = 0$ and $Q' = 1$. This corresponds to choice **b**.

**Blank B:**
In Figure 2, we trace the states to find the wave form at shaded part (1) for $Q$:
1. **First clock rise:** $J=1, K=0$. This is the **Set** state. At the first clock fall, $Q$ becomes $1$.
2. **Second clock rise:** $J=0, K=1$. This is the **Reset** state. At the second clock fall, $Q$ becomes $0$.

Since $Q$ was $1$ before the second clock fall and becomes $0$ after it, the wave form drops from $1$ to $0$. This matches wave form **a**.

**Blanks C and D:**
In Figure 2, at the **third clock rise**, $J$ is given as $1$. The value of $K$ is hidden in shaded part (2). However, we can observe the output $Q$: at the third clock fall, $Q$ changes from $0$ to $1$. 
There are two possible states for $K$ that result in $Q$ becoming $1$ when $J=1$:
- If $K=0$, the state is **Set**, making $Q$ become $1$.
- If $K=1$, the state is **Toggle**, which inverts $Q$. Since $Q$ was $0$, toggling it makes $Q$ become $1$.

Thus, $K$ can be constantly $0$ or constantly $1$ during this cycle to produce the observed output. A constant $0$ corresponds to wave form **b**, and a constant $1$ corresponds to wave form **d**.
