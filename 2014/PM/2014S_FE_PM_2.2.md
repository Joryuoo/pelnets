### Explanation

To solve this question, we must determine the multiplicities for the relationships in the class diagram (Figure 2) based on the business rules provided in the scenario.

**Blank D (Relationship between `Video` and `Medium`)**
- A `Video` represents a movie title or catalog entry (e.g., "The Matrix"), containing general details like `videoTitle` and `videoCategory`.
- A `Medium` represents a specific, physical physical copy in the store (e.g., a specific DVD disc with a unique `mediumID`).
- **Multiplicity Analysis:** A single `Video` title can have zero, one, or many physical copies available in the store (multiplicity of `0..*`). Conversely, any given physical `Medium` (a specific disc) contains exactly one movie title (multiplicity of `1`). 
- Therefore, the correct multiplicities are `1` on the `Video` side and `0..*` on the `Medium` side, which corresponds to choice **a**.

**Blank E (Relationship between `Medium` and `ChargePolicy`)**
- The scenario states: *"The rental charge is determined by the medium type. For example, BD rentals are more expensive than DVD rentals."*
- `ChargePolicy` contains the pricing rules (`chargePerDay`, `finePerDay`).
- **Multiplicity Analysis:** Every physical `Medium` has a specific type, and therefore must be associated with exactly one `ChargePolicy` (`1`). Depending on the exact abstraction in the UML diagram, the relationship between a single physical item and its governing policy is represented as `1` to `1` (choice **e**).

**Why other choices are incorrect:**
- **b, d:** These choices include a `0..1` multiplicity. Suggesting a `0..1` on the `Medium` side would mean the store could only ever own a maximum of one physical copy per video title, which contradicts how a rental chain operates.
- **c:** Reversing the `1` and `0..*` would imply that one physical disc contains many movies, and a movie only exists on one disc, which is logically backwards for a rental inventory system.
