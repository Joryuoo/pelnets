### Explanation
**Blank A:** The actor on the top left interacts with use cases such as "Search for Videos", "Log-in", "Log-out", and Blank B. The text states that "A customer can log in to the application for searching the videos" and "A customer must return the rented videos". Therefore, Blank A represents the **Customer** (e). 

**Blank B:** This use case is associated with the Customer (Blank A) and has a relationship with "Pay Fines". The scenario explains that "A customer must return the rented videos" and "In case of late return, the customer has to pay the fine". This indicates the action being performed is returning videos. Thus, Blank B is **Return Videos** (h).

**Blank C:** This denotes the relationship pointing from the "Pay Fines" use case to the "Return Videos" (Blank B) use case. The text specifies fines are paid "In case of late return", meaning this action is conditional and does not happen every time a video is returned. In UML Use Case diagrams, an optional or conditional behavior is modeled with the **«extend»** (a) relationship. 
- Conversely, `«include»` (b) is used for mandatory, unconditionally required behaviors that execute every time the base use case runs.
