### Explanation
**Blank D:**
The e-mail has to be sent to different destination servers based on the domain names of the recipients' email addresses in the `To`, `Cc`, and `Bcc` fields. Let's extract the unique domain names:
- `smith@beta.com` -> beta.com
- `helal@beta.com` -> beta.com
- `jln@delta.com` -> delta.com
- `hannan@alpha.com` -> alpha.com
- `lim@gamma.org` -> gamma.org
- `leo@alpha.org` -> alpha.org

The unique destination domains are `beta.com`, `delta.com`, `alpha.com`, `gamma.org`, and `alpha.org`. This makes a total of **5** destination servers. Thus, 5 copies of the e-mail are produced.
Therefore, D = c.

**Blank E:**
When a recipient receives an e-mail, they can see the addresses listed in the `From`, `To`, and `Cc` fields. They cannot see the addresses in the `Bcc` (Blind Carbon Copy) field.
Excluding John Smith's own e-mail address, he can find the following addresses:
- From: `suman@alpha.com` (1)
- To: `helal@beta.com`, `jln@delta.com` (2)
- Cc: `hannan@alpha.com` (1)

Total e-mail addresses visible to him (excluding himself) = 1 + 2 + 1 = **4**.
Therefore, E = b.
