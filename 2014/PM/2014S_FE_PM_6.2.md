# 2014S_FE_PM_6.2

![[2014S_FE_PM_Q6_SQ2_p1.png]]

?
d

### Explanation

**Correct Answer:** d (with the maximum right-end coordinate)

**Reasoning:**
- The algorithm processes segment endpoints sorted by their coordinate values in ascending order.
- The total number of endpoints is `2*n`, stored in the `Points` array (indices 1 to `2*n`).
- The main sweeping loop is defined as `i: 1, i <= 2*n-1, 1`. This loop processes endpoints from index 1 up to `2*n-1`, intentionally **skipping the final endpoint** at index `2*n`.
- Because the endpoints are sorted in ascending order, `Points[2*n]` holds the endpoint with the absolute largest coordinate value. 
- Since every segment's right endpoint has a larger coordinate than its left endpoint, the overall maximum coordinate across all points must be a **right-end point**.
- During the loop, processing a left-end point activates the segment (`Active[s] = 1`), and processing a right-end point inactivates it (`Active[s] = 0`).
- Because the loop terminates before processing the final right-end point at `Points[2*n]`, that specific segment is never inactivated and is left in the active state. Thus, the segment with the maximum right-end coordinate remains active.

**Why other choices are incorrect:**
- **a) number 1 & b) number n:** The loop ignores the very last element based on its sorted coordinate, independent of its segment number.
- **c) with the maximum left-end coordinate:** The absolute largest coordinate in the array is guaranteed to be a right-end point. The maximum left-end point will still have its corresponding right-end point processed (since it must be larger), meaning it will successfully be inactivated.
- **e) & f) minimum left/right end coordinates:** These endpoints have the smallest values and reside at the beginning of the sorted array. They are processed normally early on, and their segments are properly inactivated.
