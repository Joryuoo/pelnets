### Explanation
Let's trace the execution of the three tasks based on priority (High, Medium, Low).
Preemptive scheduling allows a higher priority task to interrupt a lower one, but I/O operations happen concurrently if there is no I/O contention.

| Time | CPU Task | I/O Task | Remarks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-3  | High (CPU 3) | - | High finishes 1st CPU. |
| 3-5  | Medium (CPU 2) | High (I/O 2 of 5) | Medium finishes 1st CPU. High in I/O. |
| 5-6  | Low (CPU 1) | High (I/O 3/5), Med (I/O 1/6) | Low finishes 1st CPU. High and Med in I/O. |
| 6-8  | **IDLE** (2ms) | High (I/O 5/5), Med (I/O 3/6), Low (I/O 2/5) | CPU has no tasks ready. High finishes I/O at t=8. |
| 8-10 | High (CPU 2) | Med (I/O 5/6), Low (I/O 4/5) | High finishes 2nd CPU. High complete! |
| 10-11| **IDLE** (1ms) | Med (I/O 6/6), Low (I/O 5/5) | CPU has no tasks ready. Med and Low finish I/O at t=11. |
| 11-13| Medium (CPU 2) | - | Medium finishes 2nd CPU. Med complete! |
| 13-14| Low (CPU 1) | - | Low finishes 2nd CPU. Low complete! |

Total CPU idle time is 2ms (from t=6 to t=8) + 1ms (from t=10 to t=11) = 3ms.
