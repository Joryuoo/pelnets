### Explanation

The Entity-Relationship (ER) diagram multiplicities outline the cardinality between different entities. UML conventions dictate placing the multiplicity of a relationship next to the target entity (the entity on the far side of the relationship).

Let's break down the business rules given in the question:

1. **"An employee is associated with 1 program and a program is associated with at least one employee"**
   - **Employee to Program:** One employee belongs to exactly one program. This is represented by `1..1` (or `1.1`) next to the `Program` entity.
   - **Program to Employee:** A program has one or more employees. This is represented by `1..*` (or `1.*`) next to the `Employee` entity.
   - **Visualized:** `Program [1.1] ---------------- [1.*] Employee`

2. **"A program belongs to 1 college and a college has one or many programs"**
   - **Program to College:** One program belongs to exactly one college. This is represented by `1..1` next to the `College` entity.
   - **College to Program:** One college has one or more programs. This is represented by `1..*` (or `1.*`) next to the `Program` entity.
   - **Visualized:** `College [1..1] ---------------- [1.*] Program`

Putting it all together in one sequence:
```text
College [1..1] --------- [1.*] Program [1.1] --------- [1.*] Employee
```

Looking at the options provided in the image, **diagram b** perfectly matches this exact configuration:
- The multiplicity near `College` is `1..1`.
- The multiplicity near `Program` (on the college side) is `1.*`.
- The multiplicity near `Program` (on the employee side) is `1.1`.
- The multiplicity near `Employee` is `1.*`.
