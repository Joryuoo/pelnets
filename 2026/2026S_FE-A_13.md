### Explanation

Capacity planning is a systematic process aimed at ensuring an IT system has sufficient resources to handle future demands efficiently. When a new task is introduced into an already operating system, the steps for capacity planning should follow a logical sequence:

1. **Understand Current State (iii)**:
   First, you must understand the current performance information of the hardware or environment inherent to the system based on its ongoing operating status. It establishes the baseline.

2. **Gather Requirements for the New Task (iv)**:
   Next, you conduct a meeting with users to gather information about the new task. You must understand requirements such as the estimated number of processes and the required processing time.

3. **Formulate a Proposal (ii)**:
   After determining the baseline and the requirements, you consider a proposal for the necessary system configuration. This takes into account factors like the number of servers and the possibility of parallel distributed processing.

4. **Evaluate the Proposal (i)**:
   Finally, you evaluate the proposed system configuration, assess its appropriateness to see if it meets the new demands without bottlenecking, and conduct a review if necessary.

Thus, the proper chronological sequence is **(iii) $\rightarrow$ (iv) $\rightarrow$ (ii) $\rightarrow$ (i)**.
