f) A: n, B: (count ÷ (n - 1)) × 100

### Explanation
The percentile formula is given as:
$percentile = \frac{\text{number of students below}}{\text{total students} - 1} \times 100$

1. **Blank A (Upper bound of the inner loop):**
   To find the "number of students who scored below the target student", we must compare the target student's score (`arr[i]`) with the scores of **all** students in the array. Thus, the inner loop index `j` must iterate from `1` to `n` (the total number of students). This gives us `A: n`.

2. **Blank B (Calculating the percentile):**
   After the inner loop finishes, `count` holds the number of students who scored strictly less than `arr[i]`.
   Substituting this into the formula:
   $percentile = \frac{count}{n - 1} \times 100$
   In the pseudocode, division is represented by the `÷` symbol. Thus, the calculation to store in `percentiles[i]` is `(count ÷ (n - 1)) × 100`. This gives us `B: (count ÷ (n - 1)) × 100`.

Let's test with the provided example: `arr = {30, 60, 80, 72, 15}` and $n = 5$.
For $i = 1$ (`arr[1] = 30`):
- `count` becomes `1` (only `15` is smaller).
- Percentile = $(1 \div 4) \times 100 = 25$.

This confirms that **f** is the correct combination.
