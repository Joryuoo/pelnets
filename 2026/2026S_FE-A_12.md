# 2026S_FE-A_12

Q12. Which of the following is the RAID level that can maximize reliability and fault tolerance for a NAS device equipped with two hard disk drives of identical capacity?

a) RAID 0
b) RAID 1
c) RAID 5
d) RAID 6
?
b) RAID 1

### Explanation

RAID (Redundant Array of Independent Disks) employs multiple disk drives to achieve data redundancy, performance improvement, or both. Let us evaluate the RAID levels given in the context of **two hard disk drives** and **maximizing fault tolerance**:

| RAID Level | Description | Minimum Disks Required | Fault Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RAID 0** | Striping without parity. Data is divided into blocks and spread across multiple disks. | 2 | None. If one disk fails, all data is lost. |
| **RAID 1** | Mirroring. Data is written identically to two (or more) drives. | 2 | Excellent. If one disk fails, the other contains an exact copy of the data. |
| **RAID 5** | Striping with distributed parity. | 3 | Good. Tolerates a single drive failure. |
| **RAID 6** | Striping with double distributed parity. | 4 | Excellent. Tolerates two simultaneous drive failures. |

Since the NAS device is only equipped with exactly **two** hard disk drives, RAID 5 and RAID 6 are not possible (they require at least 3 and 4 disks respectively). 

Between the remaining options (RAID 0 and RAID 1), **RAID 1** provides data redundancy and maximizes reliability, whereas RAID 0 offers no fault tolerance.
