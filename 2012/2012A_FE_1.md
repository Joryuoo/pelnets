### Explanation
In floating-point number representation (such as IEEE 754), a 32-bit floating-point number is partitioned into three fields:
- **Sign ($S$)**: 1 bit (determines positive or negative sign).
- **Exponent ($E$)**: Determines the **range** of representable values (the magnitude of numbers from smallest non-zero positive number to largest representable number).
- **Fraction / Mantissa ($F$)**: Determines the **precision** (number of significant binary digits).

Comparing Computer A and Computer B:
- **Computer A**: $E = 8\text{ bits}$, $F = 23\text{ bits}$.
- **Computer B**: $E = 7\text{ bits}$, $F = 24\text{ bits}$.

1. **Range**: Computer A allocates 8 bits for the exponent, whereas Computer B allocates 7 bits. Since Computer A has more exponent bits ($2^8 = 256$ possible exponent patterns vs. $2^7 = 128$), Computer A can represent both much larger and much smaller (closer to zero) numbers than Computer B. Therefore, the **range of Computer A is greater than that of Computer B**.
2. **Precision**: Computer B allocates 24 bits for the fraction, whereas Computer A allocates 23 bits. More bits in the mantissa allow finer granularity between consecutive numbers, providing greater significant precision. Therefore, the **precision of Computer A is smaller than that of Computer B**.

Thus, the range of Computer A is greater than that of Computer B, but the precision is smaller, making **Choice c** the correct answer.
