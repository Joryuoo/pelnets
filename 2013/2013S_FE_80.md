a) Both companies have the same break-even point and fixed cost, so the profit and loss is also equal as long as the sales are equal.
b) The break-even points of both companies are equal, so the sales necessary for making equal profits are also equal for both companies.

c) The variable cost ratio of Company A is lower than Company B, and therefore, when the sales above the break-even point are equal, the profit of Company A is higher than Company B.
### Explanation
By examining the graphs for Company A and Company B:
1. Both companies share the exact same **break-even point** (sales of 100).
2. **Company A** has fixed costs of 30, while **Company B** has fixed costs of 70. 
3. Because Company A reaches the same break-even sales volume despite starting with a lower fixed cost, its income line rises faster relative to its total cost line compared to Company B. This indicates that Company A has lower variable costs per unit (a lower variable cost ratio), yielding a higher contribution margin.
4. Consequently, once sales exceed the break-even point, Company A's profit grows at a steeper rate than Company B's, making Company A's profit higher for any given sales amount above 100.
