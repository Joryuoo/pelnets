### Explanation
**(1) Blank A: b**
Given $p = 11$ and $q = 3$:
1. $n = p \times q = 11 \times 3 = 33$.
2. $phi = (p - 1)(q - 1) = 10 \times 2 = 20$.
3. Choose $e$ such that $1 < e < 20$ and $\text{gcd}(e, 20) = 1$.
   Possible values for $e$: 3, 7, 9, 11, 13, 17, 19.
4. Compute $d$ such that $(d \times e) \bmod 20 = 1$.
   Checking the given answer choices for $(n, e)$ and $(n, d)$:
   - **a) (33, 3) and (33, 5):** $e=3, d=5 \implies 3 \times 5 = 15 \equiv 15 \pmod{20}$. Not 1.
   - **b) (33, 3) and (33, 7):** $e=3, d=7 \implies 3 \times 7 = 21 \equiv 1 \pmod{20}$. This is correct.
   - **c) (33, 7) and (33, 7):** $e=7, d=7 \implies 7 \times 7 = 49 \equiv 9 \pmod{20}$. Not 1.
   - **d) (33, 7) and (33, 9):** $e=7, d=9 \implies 7 \times 9 = 63 \equiv 3 \pmod{20}$. Not 1.

**(2) Blank B: c**
We need the number of possible key pairs $(e, d)$ under the condition that both $e < 10$ and $d < 10$.
From the previous step, possible values for $e < 10$ are 3, 7, and 9 (since $\text{gcd}(e, 20) = 1$).
Let's find the corresponding $d$ for each:
- For $e = 3$: $3 \times d \equiv 1 \pmod{20} \implies d = 7$ (since $3 \times 7 = 21$). Both $e$ and $d$ are $< 10$. (Pair 1)
- For $e = 7$: $7 \times d \equiv 1 \pmod{20} \implies d = 3$ (since $7 \times 3 = 21$). Both $e$ and $d$ are $< 10$. (Pair 2)
- For $e = 9$: $9 \times d \equiv 1 \pmod{20} \implies d = 9$ (since $9 \times 9 = 81 \equiv 1 \pmod{20}$). Both $e$ and $d$ are $< 10$. (Pair 3)

Thus, there are exactly 3 possible key pairs. Choice **c** is correct.

**(3) Blank C: b**
Given plain message $M = 7$, public key $(n, e) = (15, 3)$.
Cipher message $C = M^e \bmod n$.
$C = 7^3 \bmod 15 = 343 \bmod 15$.
Since $15 \times 22 = 330$, we have $343 - 330 = 13$.
Therefore, $C = 13$. Choice **b** is correct.
