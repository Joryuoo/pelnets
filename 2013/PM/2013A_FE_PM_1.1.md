### Explanation

**Blank A:**
A data hazard creates a bubble if an instruction needs a register whose result isn't ready. The execute (X) stage of the preceding instruction must finish before the decode (D) stage of the succeeding instruction can begin.
Let's trace the instructions:
- **I4 (R5 &larr; R2 &divide; R1):** Needs R1 from I2. I2's X stage is at cycle 3, so I4's D stage can start at cycle 4. No bubble needed.
- **I5 (R0 &larr; R5):** Needs R5 from I4. I4's X stage is at cycle 5. I5's D stage must start at cycle 6 (instead of 5). **1 bubble** is inserted at cycle 5.
- **I6 (R2 &larr; R4 &times; R5):** Needs R4 from I3 (ready) and R5 from I4 (ready). D stage is at cycle 7. No bubble.
- **I7 (R7 &larr; R7 &minus; 1):** Needs R7 from I1 (ready). D stage is at cycle 8. No bubble.
- **I8 (GOTO I2 IF R7 > 0):** Needs R7 from I7. I7's X stage is at cycle 9. I8's D stage must start at cycle 10 (instead of 9). **1 bubble** is inserted at cycle 9.
Total bubbles = 1 + 1 = **2 bubbles**. The correct choice is **c**.

**Blank B:**
I8 causes a branch. According to the text, if the branch target is not in the BTB (which is initially empty), the fetch (F) stage of the target starts after the write-back (W) stage of the branch instruction.
- I8 trace: F at 7, D at 10 (due to bubbles), X at 11, W at 12.
- The target instruction I2 starts its F stage after I8's W stage, which is at cycle **13**.
The correct choice is **e**.

**Blank C:**
If I6 and I7 are exchanged:
- New I6 (R7 &larr; R7 &minus; 1): Its X stage is now at cycle 8 (was cycle 9).
- New I7 (R2 &larr; R4 &times; R5): Its X stage is at cycle 9.
- I8 (GOTO I2 IF R7 > 0): Needs R7 from the new I6. Since new I6's X stage finishes at cycle 8, I8's D stage can naturally start at cycle 9. 
The bubble that was previously needed for I8 is eliminated. The total number of bubbles decreases by **1**.
The correct choice is **b**.

**Blank D:**
The base CPI is 1. We must add the delays caused by hazards.
- **Data hazard delay:** 25% of instructions cause a data hazard, which we can assume adds 1 bubble. Delay = 0.25 &times; 1 = 0.25.
- **Control hazard delay:** 20% of instructions are branches.
  - 75% BTB hit ratio means 25% are BTB misses.
  - On a BTB miss, the target's fetch starts after the branch's write-back, inserting 3 bubbles (B, B, B in the given diagram for T1 case 1).
  - Delay = 0.20 &times; 0.25 &times; 3 = 0.15.
- **Effective CPI** = 1 (base) + 0.25 (data) + 0.15 (control) = **1.4**.
The correct choice is **b**.
