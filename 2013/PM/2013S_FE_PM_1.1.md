### Explanation

From the problem description and truth table, the "Win" cases (where Output is 1) are:
- (1) X is ON, Y is OFF, Z is OFF: `X AND NOT(Y) AND NOT(Z)`
- (2) X is ON, Y is OFF, Z is ON: `X AND NOT(Y) AND Z`
- (3) X is OFF, Y is ON, Z is OFF: `NOT(X) AND Y AND NOT(Z)`

To find the correct logic expression, we can combine these win cases using OR (+):
Output = `(X AND NOT(Y) AND NOT(Z)) + (X AND NOT(Y) AND Z) + (NOT(X) AND Y AND NOT(Z))`

We can simplify this expression using Boolean algebra. Notice that the first two terms share `X AND NOT(Y)`. We can factor it out:
Output = `X AND NOT(Y) AND (NOT(Z) + Z) + NOT(X) AND Y AND NOT(Z)`

Since `NOT(Z) + Z` always equals 1:
Output = `X AND NOT(Y) AND (1) + NOT(X) AND Y AND NOT(Z)`
Output = `X AND NOT(Y) + NOT(X) AND Y AND NOT(Z)`

This matches option **a**.

**Why the other choices are incorrect:**
- **b)** `X AND NOT(Y) AND Z + NOT(X) AND NOT(Z)`: This expression would output 1 when all switches are OFF (`X=0, Y=0, Z=0`). According to the truth table, this should be a "Lose" condition.
- **c)** `X AND NOT(Y) AND Z + X AND Y`: This would output 1 when `X=1, Y=1, Z=0`. The truth table shows this is a "Lose" condition.
- **d)** `X AND Y AND NOT(Z) + NOT(X) AND Z`: This would output 1 when `X=0, Y=0, Z=1`. The truth table shows this is a "Lose" condition.
