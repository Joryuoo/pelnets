### Explanation

**Blank C:**  
The SQL structure used for conditional logic is the `CASE` statement. The syntax is `CASE WHEN condition THEN result ... ELSE default END`. Therefore, the correct keyword is **case** (Choice a).

**Blank D:**  
This blank calculates the overtime pay multiplier for an employee who has worked more than 4 hours (`WHEN a.OTHour > 4`). The pay structure is split into three tiers:
1. **First 2 hours (18:00 - 20:00):** Normal rate (multiplier of `1.0`), which is `2`.
2. **Next 2 hours (20:00 - 22:00):** 1.3 times the normal rate, which is `2 * 1.3`.
3. **Remaining hours (Beyond 22:00):** 1.5 times the normal rate, which applies to hours over 4, represented by `(a.OTHour - 4) * 1.5`.

Adding these together gives the formula: `2 + 2 * 1.3 + (a.OTHour - 4) * 1.5`. This matches **Choice b**.

**Blank E:**  
The preceding condition handled `a.OTHour > 4`. The next condition needs to handle overtime hours that are greater than 2 but less than or equal to 4 (i.e., when they reach the second tier of pay). Overtime is rounded to 30-minute increments, so exact boundaries should be used. The condition `a.OTHour > 2 AND a.OTHour <= 4` correctly captures this range. This matches **Choice c**.
*(Note: Choice a is too broad because it would include hours <= 2 which fall into the `ELSE` block, and Choices b and d fail to account for fractional hours like 2.5 or 3.5).*

**Blank F:**  
This blank calculates the pay multiplier for the condition where `a.OTHour` is between 2 and 4. 
1. **First 2 hours:** Normal rate, giving `2`.
2. **Remaining hours (up to 4):** 1.3 times the normal rate, giving `(a.OTHour - 2) * 1.3`.

Adding these together yields `2 + (a.OTHour - 2) * 1.3`. This matches **Choice a**.
