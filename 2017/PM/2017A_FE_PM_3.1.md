### Explanation
**Blank A:** The `NetIncome` is the actual income, and `ExpectedIncome` is the planned income. The profit for a batch is calculated as `NetIncome - ExpectedIncome`. To get the total profit per course, we group by `CourseCode` and sum this difference. Thus, `SUM(B.NetIncome) - SUM(B.ExpectedIncome)` is correct.
- a) `SUM(B.ExpectedIncome) - SUM(B.NetIncome)` would calculate the net loss, not the net profit.
- c, d) The alias `T` represents the result of the subquery itself and cannot be referenced inside the subquery's own `SELECT` clause. We must refer to the source table `BATCH` using its alias `B`.

**Blank B:** This is the `WHERE` condition that joins the result of the subquery `T` (which contains `CourseCode` and `Profit`) with the `COURSE` table `C`. The condition must link them by their common column `CourseCode`. Hence, `C.CourseCode = T.CourseCode`.
- a) `BatchCode` doesn't exist in `T` (since we grouped by `CourseCode`), and `B` is not accessible in the outer query.
- b, c) `B` is the alias used strictly inside the subquery and is out of scope in the main, outer query.
