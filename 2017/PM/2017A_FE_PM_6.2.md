### Explanation

The modified program `TrashGen2` adds logic to find the maximum possible garbage sum `M` (that is `≤ T`) among all feasible trips, and records the starting and ending portions `From` and `To` for that maximum sum trip.

Looking at the sample case in Figure 1:
`N = 9`, `T = 10`
`X = [11, 1, 2, 1, 1, 5, 10, 2, 3]`

Let's find the valid contiguous subarray (trip) with the sum closest to, but not exceeding, 10.
- Trip from portion 2 to 6: `X[2..6]` sum is `1 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 5 = 10`
- Trip from portion 7 alone: `X[7]` is `10`

The program specifically updates the maximum values using the strict inequality `Sum > M`. 
- `M` starts at 0.
- When it evaluates the trip from portion 2 to 6, `Sum` is 10. `10 > 0` is true, so `M` becomes 10, `From` becomes 2, `To` becomes 6.
- Later, when it evaluates portion 7 alone, `Sum` is 10. The condition `Sum > M` (which is `10 > 10`) evaluates to false, so `From` and `To` are **not** updated.

So the maximum sum recorded is 10, and the recorded portion range is from 2 to 6.

- Blank D corresponds to `To`, which is 6. The answer for D is **f**.
- Blank E corresponds to `M` (the max sum), which is 10. The answer for E is **j**.
