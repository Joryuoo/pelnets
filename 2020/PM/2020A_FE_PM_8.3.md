### Explanation

To trace the number of times `getResolution`, `getScreenSize`, and `getPrice` are called in `Test2`, we need to look at how `AndFilter` and `OrFilter` process the `TVS` set. The set `Test.TVS` contains 5 TVs:
1. `TY-987F`, 1080p, 50-inch, $299
2. `SX-4567`, 4K, 45-inch, $599
3. `SX-5678`, 4K, 50-inch, $759
4. `SX-6789`, 4K, 65-inch, $999
5. `SZ-8765`, 8K, 45-inch, $749

The `AndFilter` is applied to these 5 TVs. It contains 3 filters in its `filters` array:
1. `OrFilter` (which checks for "4K" or "1080p")
2. `RangeFilter` for screen size (45 to 55)
3. `RangeFilter` for price (0 to 750)

In `AndFilter.applyFilter`:
`filteredTvs` initially has 5 TVs.
**Step 1:** The first filter (`OrFilter`) is applied to `filteredTvs` (size 5).
In `OrFilter.applyFilter(tvs)` (where `tvs` has 5 elements):
- It applies the first equality filter ("4K") to all 5 TVs. `Tv::getResolution` is called 5 times.
- It applies the second equality filter ("1080p") to all 5 TVs. `Tv::getResolution` is called another 5 times.
Total calls to `getResolution`: 5 + 5 = 10 times. (Option **g**)
The result from `OrFilter` is TVs that are 4K or 1080p. The 8K TV (SZ-8765) is excluded.
The union contains 4 TVs.
`filteredTvs.retainAll(result)` reduces `filteredTvs` to these 4 TVs.

**Step 2:** The second filter (`RangeFilter` for screen size) is applied to the new `filteredTvs` (size 4).
In `RangeFilter.applyFilter`:
It calls `getter.apply(tv)` for each TV. The condition is `lo <= getter.apply(tv) && getter.apply(tv) <= hi`. Note that `getter.apply(tv)` is called **twice** per TV if the first condition is true.
For the 4 TVs left, `lo <= getter.apply(tv)` is true for all of them (they are all $\ge$ 45), so the second condition `getter.apply(tv) <= hi` is also evaluated.
Thus, for 4 TVs, `Tv::getScreenSize` is called 4 * 2 = 8 times. (Option **f**)
TVs left:
- `TY-987F` (50) -> passed
- `SX-4567` (45) -> passed
- `SX-5678` (50) -> passed
- `SX-6789` (65) -> failed
The result contains 3 TVs. `filteredTvs` is reduced to 3 TVs.

**Step 3:** The third filter (`RangeFilter` for price) is applied to `filteredTvs` (size 3).
Similarly, `getter.apply(tv)` is called twice per TV in `RangeFilter` if the first condition is true. For the 3 remaining TVs, the price is $\ge$ 0, so both bounds are checked.
For 3 TVs, `Tv::getPrice` is called 3 * 2 = 6 times. (Option **e**)
(TVs with price <= 750 pass, `SX-5678` fails, remaining 2 TVs are returned).

Therefore:
- `getResolution` (Blank E): 10 times (g)
- `getScreenSize` (Blank F): 8 times (f)
- `getPrice` (Blank G): 6 times (e)
