### Explanation
**Blank D:** The SQL statement joins the Customer and Examination tables. Since a customer might have taken multiple examinations, multiple rows per customer are returned if they have multiple records in the Examination table. 
- Using `COUNT(Customer.CustomerID)` counts every row returned by the join, leading to duplicates if a baby took multiple exams.
- To count each customer exactly once regardless of how many exams they took, we must count the distinct customer IDs.
- Changing it to `COUNT(DISTINCT Customer.CustomerID)` resolves this issue (option **d**).
