### Explanation

**Subquestion 2: Selecting Pilots for Special Flight**

The airline needs to find pilots who are certified to operate an airplane capable of flying 3200 km or farther, and at least one such airplane must be an Airbus.

Let's break down `SQL2`:
```sql
SELECT DISTINCT Name 
FROM Employee E, Certificate C 
WHERE E.EmployeeCode = C.EmployeeCode 
  AND AirplaneCode IN (SELECT A.AirplaneCode FROM Airplane A 
                       WHERE Distance >= 3200 ) 
  AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM Airplane A 
              WHERE C.AirplaneCode = A.AirplaneCode  
                AND Type LIKE 'Airbus%' ) 
```

1. **Blank C (`Distance >= 3200`)**:
   - The first condition requires the pilot to operate an airplane that can fly 3200 km or farther. 
   - The subquery selects `AirplaneCode` from the `Airplane` table where this condition is met. This filters `C.AirplaneCode` to only those planes with a sufficient maximum range.
   - This corresponds to answer **(c)**.

2. **Blanks D and E (`EXISTS` and `C.AirplaneCode = A.AirplaneCode`)**:
   - The query needs to verify that the airplane identified by the certificate `C` is indeed an Airbus.
   - Blank D uses the `EXISTS` keyword to check if a record is returned by the correlated subquery.
   - Blank E establishes the correlation by linking the outer query's certificate to the subquery's airplane (`C.AirplaneCode = A.AirplaneCode`). Together, they check if the specific airplane code in the current certificate record belongs to an Airbus (`Type LIKE 'Airbus%'`).
   - Because the outer query selects `DISTINCT Name` based on certificates `C` that satisfy *both* the range condition (via `IN`) and the make condition (via `EXISTS`), it successfully identifies pilots like La Que and Quan Cam Ly who hold at least one certificate for an Airbus capable of flying over 3200 km.
   - This corresponds to answers **(a)** and **(a)**.
