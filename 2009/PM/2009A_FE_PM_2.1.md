### Explanation
This problem asks us to generalize the River Crossing puzzle for $n$ animals where $T_k$ eats $T_{k-1}$ but is eaten by $T_{k+1}$. To ensure no animals eat each other, we must never leave consecutive animals (e.g., $T_1$ and $T_2$) alone together on either side of the river.

Notice that if we group all the **even-indexed animals** together ($T_2, T_4, \dots$) or all the **odd-indexed animals** together ($T_1, T_3, \dots$), they will not eat each other because there are no consecutive indices in either group.

The boat can carry up to $m = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ animals. This is exactly the number of even-indexed animals, and also the number of odd-indexed animals (except if $n$ is odd, in which case there is one extra odd-indexed animal, $T_{2m+1}$).

Let's trace the steps:
- **Step 1 (Blank A):** We need to take a safe group to the Left side. If we take all the even-indexed animals (**h: $T_2, T_4, \dots, T_{2m}$**), we leave behind only odd-indexed animals on the Right side. Both sides are safe.
- **Step 2:** Go back alone.
- **Step 3 (Blank B):** We take the first $m$ odd-indexed animals (**g: $T_1, T_3, \dots, T_{2m-1}$**) to the Left side. 
- **Step 4:** If $n$ is even, all animals are on the Left side, and we stop. If $n$ is odd, one animal ($T_{2m+1}$) is left on the Right side, so we proceed to Step 5.
- **Step 5 (Blank C):** We arrived on the Left side with both even and odd animals, so they will eat each other if the owner leaves. To fix this, the owner must take the even-indexed animals (**h: $T_2, T_4, \dots, T_{2m}$**) back to the Right side.
- **Step 6 (Blank D):** Now on the Right side, the owner takes the remaining odd-indexed animal (**d: $T_{2m+1}$**) to the Left side. 
- **Step 7:** Go back alone. The Right side only has even-indexed animals, and the Left side only has odd-indexed animals. Both are safe.
- **Step 8 (Blank E):** Finally, the owner takes the even-indexed animals (**h: $T_2, T_4, \dots, T_{2m}$**) to the Left side. All $n$ animals are now on the Left side with the owner.
