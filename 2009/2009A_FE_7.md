A loss of trailing digits (or loss of precision) in floating-point addition or subtraction occurs when the difference between the exponents of the two operands is very large. To perform the operation, the computer must align the decimal points by shifting the mantissa of the smaller number to the right. 

If the shift amount exceeds the bit-width of the mantissa (in this case, 23 bits), the significant bits of the smaller number will fall off the end and be lost or truncated.

Let's evaluate the difference in exponents for each choice:
- **a)** `-15 - (-16) = 1` bit shift. No significant loss.
- **b)** `16 - 16 = 0` bit shift. No shift required.
- **c)** `18 - (-5) = 23` bit shifts. The smaller number is shifted right by 23 bits. Since the mantissa is only 23 bits, the smaller number's significant bits will be shifted out and lost.
- **d)** `21 - 20 = 1` bit shift. No significant loss.

Therefore, expression **c** will result in a loss of trailing digits.
