# 2009A_FE_10

A “negative AND” operation “X NAND Y” of X and Y is defined as “NOT (X AND Y)”. Which of the following is the logical expression that represents “X OR Y” by using NAND only?

a) ((X NAND Y) NAND X) NAND Y
b) (X NAND X) NAND (Y NAND Y)
c) (X NAND Y) NAND (X NAND Y)
d) X NAND (Y NAND (X NAND Y))
?
b) (X NAND X) NAND (Y NAND Y)

### Explanation
### Explanation
By De Morgan's laws, the OR operation can be expressed using NOT and AND:
`X OR Y = NOT (NOT X AND NOT Y)`

Using the definition of NAND (`A NAND B = NOT (A AND B)`):
1. A NOT operation can be created by NANDing a variable with itself: 
   `NOT X = X NAND X`
   `NOT Y = Y NAND Y`

2. Substituting these into our De Morgan's expression gives:
   `X OR Y = NOT ((X NAND X) AND (Y NAND Y))`

3. The outer `NOT (... AND ...)` is exactly the definition of a NAND operation between the two parts:
   `X OR Y = (X NAND X) NAND (Y NAND Y)`

This is identical to choice **b**.
