# 2025A_FE-A_12

![[2025A_FE-A_Q12_full.png]]

d) $a(1-(1-b)^3)(1-(1-c)^2)$

### Explanation
To find the total availability of the system, we need to calculate the availability of each required component and then multiply them together, as they must all operate successfully for the system to be considered available.

The system is available if:
1. The **Server** is running.
2. The **LAN** is running.
3. **At least one (1) of the three (3) Clients** is running.
4. **At least one (1) of the two (2) Printers** is running.

**Step-by-step Calculation:**
1. **Server Availability**: The server is a single point of failure. Its availability is simply $a$.
2. **LAN Availability**: The LAN is also a single point of failure. Its availability is $1$.
3. **Client Subsystem Availability**: The system needs at least 1 of 3 clients to run (parallel configuration).
   - Probability that a single client fails = $(1 - b)$
   - Probability that **all 3** clients fail = $(1 - b)^3$
   - Probability that **at least 1** client works = $1 - (1 - b)^3$
4. **Printer Subsystem Availability**: The system needs at least 1 of 2 printers to run (parallel configuration).
   - Probability that a single printer fails = $(1 - c)$
   - Probability that **all 2** printers fail = $(1 - c)^2$
   - Probability that **at least 1** printer works = $1 - (1 - c)^2$

**Total System Availability**:
Since the server, the LAN, the client subsystem, and the printer subsystem are in series, we multiply their availabilities:
$$Total Availability = a \times 1 \times [1 - (1 - b)^3] \times [1 - (1 - c)^2]$$
$$= a(1 - (1 - b)^3)(1 - (1 - c)^2)$$

This matches option **d**.
