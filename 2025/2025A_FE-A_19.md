a) OrderCode $\rightarrow$ CustomerCode $\rightarrow$ CustomerAddress

### Explanation
A **transitive functional dependency** occurs when a non-key attribute depends on another non-key attribute, which in turn depends on the primary key. Mathematically, if $X \rightarrow Y$ and $Y \rightarrow Z$ (where $Y$ does not determine $X$), then $X \rightarrow Z$ is a transitive dependency ($X \rightarrow Y \rightarrow Z$).

Let's evaluate the functional dependencies given in the problem:
1. `OrderCode` $\rightarrow$ `CustomerCode` (extracted from `OrderCode` $\rightarrow$ `{..., CustomerCode, ...}`)
2. `CustomerCode` $\rightarrow$ `CustomerAddress` (extracted from `CustomerCode` $\rightarrow$ `{..., CustomerAddress}`)

Since `OrderCode` functionally determines `CustomerCode`, and `CustomerCode` functionally determines `CustomerAddress`, we have a valid transitive chain:
**`OrderCode` $\rightarrow$ `CustomerCode` $\rightarrow$ `CustomerAddress`**. This perfectly matches option (a).

Checking other options:
- (b) `OrderCode` $\rightarrow$ `ProductCode` does not exist. `OrderCode` and `ProductCode` are parts of a composite key for order details, they don't determine each other.
- (c) `SupplierCode` $\rightarrow$ `ProductSalePrice` does not exist.
- (d) `SupplierManagerCode` $\rightarrow$ `SupplierAddress` is false. The dependency is `SupplierCode` $\rightarrow$ `SupplierAddress` and `SupplierCode` $\rightarrow$ `SupplierManagerCode`.
