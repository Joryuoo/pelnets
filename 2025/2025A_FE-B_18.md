### Explanation
This question deals with establishing practical and secure patch management rules in a constrained environment.

1. **Revising Patch Management Rules (A):**
   Company Q cannot apply all patches within three days due to limited human resources and a three-day compatibility testing requirement for each component. Therefore, choices (a) and (b) are unrealistic. 
   Running a vulnerability scanner after applying patches (choices e, f) does not address the fundamental issue of when to apply the patches.
   The best approach is to adopt a risk-based strategy: prioritize and expedite patches for vulnerabilities that are actively being exploited in the wild (where "exploitation methods have been identified"). Applying these within a strict timeframe (e.g., one week) balances security needs with testing constraints. This corresponds to the first part of choices (c) and (d).

2. **Interim Protective Measures (B):**
   While waiting for compatibility testing to finish and the patch to be applied, the server remains vulnerable. Disabling the affected components (choice c) would cause the e-commerce site to go down, violating the strict Maximum Tolerable Downtime (MTD) of one hour per week. 
   Instead, deploying a **Web Application Firewall (WAF)** on the webserver can detect and block malicious traffic attempting to exploit the unpatched vulnerability, providing a layer of defense without taking the system offline.

Combining these two strategies points to choice **d**.
