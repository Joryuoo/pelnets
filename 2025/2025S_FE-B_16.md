### Explanation

This algorithm uses the Monte Carlo method to estimate the probability of winning the game of Craps.

**Step-by-Step Breakdown:**

1. **Inner `do-while` Loop (Blank A):**
   When the initial roll (`sum`) is not a definitive win (7, 11) or loss (2, 3, 12), the game continues.
   The player keeps rolling the dice (`newsum`) until they either:
   - Roll their initial `sum` again (they win: `wins_sum` increments).
   - Roll a `7` (they lose: `lose_sum` increments).
   Therefore, the loop must *continue* as long as **neither** of these terminating conditions is met.
   This means we continue `while` the new roll is not equal to `sum` AND not equal to `7`. 
   Blank **A** is: `newsum ≠ sum and newsum ≠ 7`.

2. **Calculating the Measured Probability (Blank B):**
   The variable `result` holds the measured experimental probability, $Pm$.
   Probability is calculated as the total number of successful outcomes (`wins_sum`) divided by the total number of trials (`n`).
   Blank **B** is: `wins_sum ÷ n`.

3. **Calculating Relative Error (Blank C):**
   The relative error $Er$ formula is given as $Er = |(Pm - Pt) / Pt|$.
   In the code:
   - $Pm$ is stored in `result`.
   - $Pt$ is stored in `pt`.
   The `output` statement already includes the `absolute value of` function wrapper. We just need to provide the inner expression.
   Blank **C** is: `(result - pt) ÷ pt`.
