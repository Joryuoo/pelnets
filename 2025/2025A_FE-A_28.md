### Explanation
In public key cryptography, each user has a pair of keys: a private key (kept secret) and a public key (shared openly).

For a **digital signature**:
1. **Signing**: The sender ($Y$) uses their own **private key** to encrypt the hash of the message, creating the digital signature. Since only $Y$ has their private key, this proves that the message originated from $Y$.
2. **Verification**: The receiver ($X$) uses the sender's ($Y$'s) **public key** to decrypt the digital signature and verify the hash.

Therefore, $X$ uses $Y$'s public key to verify that the message came from $Y$.
