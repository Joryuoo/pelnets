### Explanation
An **Operating Characteristic (OC) curve** describes how well an acceptance sampling plan discriminates between good and bad lots.
- **X-axis**: Percent defective in the lot ($p$).
- **Y-axis**: Probability of acceptance ($P_a$).

If the maximum allowed number of defectives (the acceptance number, $C$) increases while the sample size $n$ is held constant, the probability of accepting the lot will **increase** for any given defect rate $p$. 

This means that for the same defective percentage, a plan with $C=2$ has a higher chance of passing than a plan with $C=1$, which in turn has a higher chance of passing than $C=0$.

Visually, as $C$ increases from $0$ to $1$ to $2$, the OC curve shifts to the **right/upwards**. Therefore, the highest curve corresponds to the highest $C$ value. 
Diagram **b** correctly shows the progression where the top curve is $C=2$, the middle is $C=1$, and the bottom is $C=0$.
