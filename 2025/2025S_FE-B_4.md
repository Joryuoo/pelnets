### Explanation
The function `convert(number)` takes a binary number represented as a base-10 integer (e.g., $1100$) and converts it to its actual decimal (base-10) equivalent.

It extracts digits from right to left using `modulo 10`.
Each extracted digit corresponds to a power of $2$, starting from $2^0 = 1$. The variable `place` tracks this power of 2.

In the `while` loop:
- `remainder ← n mod 10` extracts the rightmost digit ($0$ or $1$).
- `n ← integer part of (n ÷ 10)` removes the extracted digit from `n`.
- To compute the decimal value, we add the current digit multiplied by its place value. Therefore, `A` is `remainder × place`.
- For the next iteration, the place value must double (e.g., $1 \to 2 \to 4 \to 8$). Therefore, `B` is `2 × place`.

| Digit position | Digit (`remainder`) | `place` | Value Added to `decimal` (`A`) | Next `place` (`B`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (rightmost) | $d_0$ | $1$ | $d_0 \times 1$ | $1 \times 2 = 2$ |
| 1 | $d_1$ | $2$ | $d_1 \times 2$ | $2 \times 2 = 4$ |
| 2 | $d_2$ | $4$ | $d_2 \times 4$ | $4 \times 2 = 8$ |

Thus, the correct combination is `remainder × place` and `2 × place`.
