# 2025A_FE-A_30

Q30. When a fraud occurs, all three (3) elements of the “fraud triangle” are believed to exist. Which of the following is the appropriate explanation of an element of the “fraud triangle”?

a) “Information and communication” refers to the identification, understanding, and processing of the required information, and its proper communication both within and outside the organization, and among the concerned persons.
b) “Opportunity” refers to the existence of an environment, such as technologies like the information system, physical environment, organizational rules, etc., that makes fraud possible or easy to commit.
c) “Pressure,” also known as incentive or motivation, refers to the selfish reasoning to justify committing fraud, such as interpreting things in a way that overcomes guilt or shifts blame.
d) “Rationalization” refers to the feeling of being forced caused by unrealistic performance targets.
?
b) “Opportunity” refers to the existence of an environment, such as technologies like the information system, physical environment, organizational rules, etc., that makes fraud possible or easy to commit.

### Explanation
The "Fraud Triangle" is a model for explaining the factors that cause someone to commit occupational fraud. It consists of three components:
1. **Opportunity**: The circumstances that allow fraud to occur. A weak internal control environment or lack of oversight creates the opportunity for someone to commit and conceal fraud. (Choice b is correct).
2. **Pressure (Incentive/Motivation)**: The motivation behind the crime, often financial pressure or unrealistic performance targets. Choice (c) incorrectly defines pressure as selfish reasoning to justify committing fraud (this is Rationalization). Choice (d) incorrectly defines Rationalization as feeling forced due to unrealistic targets (this is Pressure).
3. **Rationalization**: The mindset or justification that the fraudster uses to convince themselves that the act is acceptable (e.g., "I'm just borrowing the money," or "I deserve a raise").

Choice (a) refers to a component of the COSO internal control framework, not the Fraud Triangle.
