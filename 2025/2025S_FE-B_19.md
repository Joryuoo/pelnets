### Explanation

The incident occurred because human error allowed an internal document to be published externally. An administrator uploaded the wrong file, and the secondary administrator failed to notice the mistake during the verification process.

**Preventing Similar Issues (Blank A):**
- Relying on human verification has already proven to be ineffective (the second admin missed it). 
- To actively *prevent* the issue at the source, a technical control should be implemented. 
- Since all internal documents are guaranteed to have the "For Internal Use" watermark, the most effective preventative measure is deploying **an automated tool to prevent the upload of files containing [this] watermark to the website's public directory**. This eliminates the reliance on human diligence.

**Quickly Detecting Issues (Blank B):**
- "Detection" implies finding a problem *after* it has occurred (or slipped past preventative controls). 
- To quickly identify if an internal file somehow bypassed the upload filters and made it to the public directory, the security team should implement **periodic checks (or scans) of the files in the public directory to identify any files that may be mistakenly exposed**.
