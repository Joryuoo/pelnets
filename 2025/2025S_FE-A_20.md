### Explanation
Let's analyze the business rules provided in the conditions to determine the correct multiplicities for `I` (Department to Assignment history) and `II` (Employee to Assignment history).

- **Condition (1): "One or more employees belong to a department."**
  This means that a `Department` must have at least one active employee. Because every employee's department assignment is recorded in the `Assignment history` (as per Condition 3), a `Department` will have at least one corresponding `Assignment history` record. Furthermore, a department will have multiple such records over time as employees join and leave. Therefore, the multiplicity `I` on the `Assignment history` side from `Department` must be **`1..*`** (one or more).

- **Condition (2): "An employee belongs to any one department."**
  This means an active employee is always assigned to a department.

- **Condition (3): "The history of the departments to which an employee has belonged is recorded as the assignment history."**
  Since an employee must belong to a department (Condition 2), there must be at least one record of this assignment in the `Assignment history`. If the employee changes departments over time, there will be multiple records. Therefore, an `Employee` will have at least one and potentially many `Assignment history` records. This makes the multiplicity `II` on the `Assignment history` side from `Employee` **`1..*`** (one or more).

Both blanks `I` and `II` require the `1..*` multiplicity.
