### Explanation

The scenario describes a credential stuffing or password spraying attack where an attacker successfully compromised an account because the employee reused their complex password across multiple external web services.

**Preventing Future Compromise (Blank A):**
- The password policy was already strong (complex passwords), but it couldn't stop the attack because the password was compromised elsewhere and reused. 
- Making the password policy even *more* complex will not solve password reuse.
- The most robust way to prevent account compromise from stolen passwords is to require a second form of verification that the attacker cannot easily obtain from a data breach. Thus, implementing **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) to all employee accounts** is the correct preventative measure.

**Quickly Detecting Compromise (Blank B):**
- The logs showed 1,000 failed login attempts originating from a foreign country where the company has no offices and no traveling employees.
- To detect this type of anomalous activity quickly, the company should configure their monitoring system to alert on abnormal geographic logins. This is often referred to as "Impossible Travel" or anomalous location detection.
- Therefore, the system should alert administrators to **login attempts from locations where the account is not usually logged in.**
