# 2010A_FE_1

There is an 8-bit register where integers are represented in binary by using 2’s complement for negative numbers.  When the decimal integer “–24” is stored in the register and then arithmetically shifted 2 bits right, what is the resulting value in decimal?  Here, the leftmost bit of the register is used as a sign bit.

a) –102
b) –96
c) –6
d) 58
?
c) –6

### Explanation
The number -24 in 8-bit 2's complement binary:
1. Positive 24 is `0001 1000`
2. Invert bits: `1110 0111`
3. Add 1: `1110 1000` (-24)

An arithmetic shift right by 2 bits shifts all bits to the right by 2 positions, copying the sign bit (the leftmost bit, `1`) into the newly vacated positions on the left.
`1110 1000` shifted right by 2 bits becomes `1111 1010`.

Now, convert `1111 1010` back to decimal:
1. Since the sign bit is `1`, it is negative.
2. Invert bits: `0000 0101`
3. Add 1: `0000 0110` (6)
Therefore, the resulting decimal value is -6.
