### Explanation
A queue operates on a First-In-First-Out (FIFO) basis. Elements are inserted at the tail (enqueue) and deleted from the head (dequeue).

In a singly linked list:
- Deleting at the head is an $O(1)$ operation because we have direct access via the head pointer and can simply update it to point to the next node.
- Inserting at the tail is an $O(n)$ operation if we only have a head pointer, because we must traverse the entire list to find the last node. However, if we also maintain a **Tail pointer**, inserting at the tail becomes an $O(1)$ operation since we can directly append a node after the tail and update the tail pointer.

Therefore, the most efficient structure is to maintain both a Head pointer and a Tail pointer, with the pointers directed from the head towards the tail so that the list structure is preserved when the head is removed.
