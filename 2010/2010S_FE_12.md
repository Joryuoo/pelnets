# 2010S_FE_12

Q12. When the binary search algorithm is applied to the list of data sorted in ascending order 
as shown below, how many comparisons are needed to find the fact that “104” is not in 
the list?  
 
5  7  20  33  44  46  48  99  101  102  105  
 
a) 3 
b) 4 
c) 5 
d) 6
?
b) 4

### Explanation
### Explanation
There are 11 elements in the sorted list. A binary search repeatedly divides the search interval in half. 
Let's trace the search for "104":
1. **First comparison:** The middle element of the 11 elements is the 6th element, which is `46`. Since 104 > 46, we search the right half.
2. **Second comparison:** The right half has 5 elements (`48, 99, 101, 102, 105`). The middle element is the 3rd of this sublist, which is `101`. Since 104 > 101, we search the right half.
3. **Third comparison:** The remaining sublist is `102, 105`. The middle element (typically the first of the two when rounding down) is `102`. Since 104 > 102, we search the right half.
4. **Fourth comparison:** The remaining sublist has one element, `105`. Since 104 < 105, the search concludes that 104 is not in the list.

A total of 4 comparisons are made.
