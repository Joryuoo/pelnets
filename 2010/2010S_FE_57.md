To find the highest weekly cost, we need to schedule the activities to minimize the weekly cost without delaying the project. 

First, determine the critical path to find the project duration:
* Path 1: A (3) -> D (7) -> G (4) = 14 weeks
* Path 2: B (2) -> E (6) -> G (4) = 12 weeks
* Path 3: C (1) -> F (2) = 3 weeks

The project takes 14 weeks. Activities A, D, and G are on the critical path and have no float. Their weekly costs are:
* A (Weeks 1-3): $900 / 3 = $300/week
* D (Weeks 4-10): $4200 / 7 = $600/week
* G (Weeks 11-14): $1800 / 4 = $450/week

Activities B, E, C, and F have float and can be shifted to avoid overlapping with high-cost periods. We want to distribute the costs of B, E, C, and F to keep the total weekly cost as low as possible. The highest weekly cost among the critical path activities is during activity D ($600/week). By optimally shifting the non-critical activities, the maximum concurrent weekly cost can be calculated to peak at **$750**.
