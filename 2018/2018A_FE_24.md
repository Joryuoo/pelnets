### Explanation
In an anode-common LED display, a segment lights up when its corresponding port output bit is `0` (active-low).

Writing hexadecimal $92_{16}$ to the port gives binary $10010010_2$:
- $P_7 (\text{Dt}) = 1 \implies \text{OFF}$
- $P_6 (\text{g}) = 0 \implies \text{ON}$
- $P_5 (\text{f}) = 0 \implies \text{ON}$
- $P_4 (\text{e}) = 1 \implies \text{OFF}$
- $P_3 (\text{d}) = 0 \implies \text{ON}$
- $P_2 (\text{c}) = 0 \implies \text{ON}$
- $P_1 (\text{b}) = 1 \implies \text{OFF}$
- $P_0 (\text{a}) = 0 \implies \text{ON}$

Segments ON: $a, c, d, f, g$. Segments OFF: $b, e$, and $\text{Dt}$.
This illuminates the digit **5** with the decimal point OFF, which corresponds to choice **c)**.
