# 2007A_FE_17

Which of the following equations holds well in the postfix notation (or Reverse Polish Notation)?  Here, x, y, and z are variables.

a) x y + z - = x y z + -
b) x y - z + = x y z - +
c) x y - z + = x y z - -
d) x y - z - = x y z - -
?
c) x y - z + = x y z - -

### Explanation
In postfix (Reverse Polish) notation, an operator acts on the two most recently evaluated operands.
Let's evaluate both sides of the correct equation (choice c):
- **Left Side:** `x y - z +`
  1. `x y -` evaluates to `(x - y)`
  2. `... z +` adds `z` to the previous result, yielding `(x - y) + z`
- **Right Side:** `x y z - -`
  1. The first operator `-` applies to `y` and `z`, evaluating to `(y - z)`
  2. The second operator `-` applies to `x` and the result of step 1, yielding `x - (y - z)`
  
By algebra, `x - (y - z)` simplifies to `x - y + z`, which exactly matches the left side.

Testing other choices will reveal that they do not algebraically balance.
