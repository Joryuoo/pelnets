Because of referential integrity (foreign key constraints), a record cannot be deleted from a parent table if there are related records in a child table. To remove project records without violating these constraints, the deletion must happen from the bottom of the hierarchy upwards. 

The hierarchy is `Projects` (parent) -> `Project_Modules` (child of Projects) -> `Work_Done` (child of Project_Modules).

1. First, delete related records in `Work_Done` (Statement 2).
2. Second, delete related records in `Project_Modules` (Statement 4).
3. Finally, delete the record in `Projects` (Statement 1).

This gives the correct execution sequence of 2 -> 4 -> 1.

- **(a), (b), and (c)** are incorrect because they attempt to delete parent records before their corresponding child records, which would result in foreign key constraint violations. Furthermore, Statement 3 uses incorrect join syntax that isn't standard or required when sequential deletes are provided.
