### Explanation
The goal is to extract the names "Honey" and "Rob" from the `Persons` table based on their attributes.
* **Honey:** age = 23, gender = 'F', country = 'Korea'
* **Rob:** age = 26, gender = 'M', country = 'Philippines'

Let's evaluate the `WHERE` clause for the correct option (b):
`SELECT name FROM Persons WHERE country LIKE '%Korea%' AND gender = 'F' OR age = 26`

Because the `AND` operator has higher precedence than `OR`, the condition is evaluated as:
`(country LIKE '%Korea%' AND gender = 'F') OR (age = 26)`

* Honey matches because her country is 'Korea' and her gender is 'F' (satisfies the first part of the `OR`).
* Rob matches because his age is 26 (satisfies the second part of the `OR`).
* No other person in the table matches either of these conditions. For example, Sakura fails both (not from Korea, age is 27), Mark fails both (gender is M, age is 22), etc.

Therefore, option b correctly selects exactly Honey and Rob.
