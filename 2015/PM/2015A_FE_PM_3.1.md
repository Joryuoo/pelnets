### Explanation
To complete the E-R diagram, we must add attributes that serve as foreign keys to establish the relationships described in Table 1. In relational databases, relationships between entities are formed by placing the primary key of one entity into the related entity. 

*   **Blank A:** The `Exercise` entity represents information about each exercise associated with a specific muscle. To link an exercise to its corresponding muscle, we must add the primary key of the `Muscle` entity, which is **`Muscle_ID` (e)**, as an attribute in `Exercise`.
*   **Blank B:** The `Muscle` entity holds the name of each muscle that belongs to a major muscle group. To link a muscle to its parent muscle group, we must add the primary key of the `Muscle_Group` entity, which is **`MG_ID` (d)**, as an attribute in `Muscle`.
*   **Blank C:** Table 1 defines `Training_Schedule` as the "Training schedule of exercises for a customer". This establishes a link between a `Customer` and an `Exercise`. Therefore, the `Training_Schedule` entity needs the primary keys of both to identify which customer is performing which exercise. Thus, `Customer_ID` and `Exercise_ID` must be added to **`Training_Schedule` (d)**.

**Incorrect Choices (for C):**
*   **a) Index / b) Index_Progress:** These relate to measuring and tracking the customer's physical progress, not the scheduling of exercises.
*   **c) Muscle_Group:** This entity only categorizes muscles and does not associate customers with specific exercises.
