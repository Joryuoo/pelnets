# 2016S_FE_27

Which of the following is more suitable for outline fonts than for bitmap fonts?

a) To display single-byte and double-byte characters at the same time
b) To display characters as quickly as possible
c) To display characters in monospace
d) To enlarge characters by a given scale factor
?
d) To enlarge characters by a given scale factor

### Explanation

Digital fonts are primarily classified into **bitmap (raster) fonts** and **outline (vector) fonts**:

1. **Outline Fonts (Vector Fonts):**
   - Characters are described mathematically using geometric primitives such as lines, Bézier curves, and control points (e.g., TrueType, OpenType, PostScript).
   - Because glyph outlines are defined by mathematical formulas rather than fixed pixels, they are **resolution-independent**.
   - They can be scaled (enlarged or shrunk) to any arbitrary scale factor or printed at high resolutions without loss of quality, blurring, or jagged edges ("jaggies").
   - This makes outline fonts ideal for **enlarging characters by a given scale factor** (**d)**.

2. **Bitmap Fonts (Raster Fonts):**
   - Characters are stored as a fixed grid/matrix of dots or pixels for each specific point size.
   - When a bitmap font is scaled up or enlarged, the individual square pixels are simply multiplied, resulting in conspicuous stair-stepping/pixelation (jagged edges).

---

#### Evaluation of Options:
- **a) is incorrect:** Both outline fonts and bitmap fonts can represent single-byte (ASCII) and double-byte (Unicode/CJK) characters simultaneously.
- **b) is incorrect:** Bitmap fonts are faster to display on screen because their pixel representations are pre-calculated and can be mapped directly to video memory without rasterization or geometric calculations.
- **c) is incorrect:** Monospaced (fixed-pitch) font designs exist in both bitmap and outline font formats.
- **d) is correct:** Outline fonts retain clean, crisp curves at any magnification scale factor.
