# 2016A_FE_29
![[2016A_FE_AM_Q29_p1.png]]

a) A: INNER JOIN, B: Amount
b) A: INNER JOIN, B: Product.PName
c) A: LEFT JOIN, B: Amount
d) A: LEFT JOIN, B: Product.PName
?
d) A: LEFT JOIN, B: Product.PName
### Explanation
Let's analyze the SQL query requirements step by step:

#### 1. Determining Box A (Join Operation):
- The problem requires listing *"all products with their sales amount, including the products that have no sales amount."*
- An **`INNER JOIN`** returns only rows with matching `ProductID` values present in **both** tables. Products that have never been sold (having no records in `Sales`) would be excluded.
- To preserve all records from the `Product` table regardless of whether they have matching sales entries, an **outer join** is required.
- In the clause `FROM Product _____A_____ Sales`, the `Product` table is the left-hand table. Therefore, a **`LEFT JOIN`** (or `LEFT OUTER JOIN`) ensures all product rows are retained, producing `NULL` for sales attributes where no sales records exist.
- Thus, **A = LEFT JOIN**.

#### 2. Determining Box B (GROUP BY Clause):
- The `SELECT` clause contains:
  ```sql
  SELECT Product.PName, SUM(Amount)
  ```
- In standard SQL, whenever an aggregate function (such as `SUM`, `COUNT`, `AVG`) is used with regular columns, **all non-aggregated columns** in the `SELECT` list must be included in the **`GROUP BY`** clause.
- Here, `Product.PName` is non-aggregated, so the query must group the records by `Product.PName` to aggregate each product's sales amounts individually.
- Placing `Amount` in the `GROUP BY` clause would be invalid because `Amount` is the argument being aggregated by `SUM()`.
- Thus, **B = Product.PName**.

#### Complete Query:
```sql
SELECT Product.PName, SUM(Amount)
FROM Product LEFT JOIN Sales
ON Product.ProductID = Sales.ProductID
GROUP BY Product.PName;
```

This corresponds to option **d)**.
