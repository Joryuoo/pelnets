# 2016A_FE_23
Which of the following is the name of a phenomenon where multiple ON/OFF signals are generated within a few milliseconds after a push-button switch with a mechanical contact is pressed once?

a) Buffering
b) Chattering
c) Sampling
d) Sharing
?
b) Chattering
### Explanation
**Chattering** (also referred to as **contact bounce**) is a physical phenomenon that occurs in electromechanical switches and relays:

- **Cause:** When a mechanical push-button switch is pressed or released, the physical metal contacts do not make or break a clean electrical contact instantaneously. Instead, the momentum and elasticity of the metal springs/contacts cause them to physically bounce against each other for a few milliseconds (typically 1 to 20 ms) before establishing a stable connection.
- **Effect:** This physical bouncing causes the electrical circuit to open and close rapidly, generating multiple unwanted ON/OFF pulses within that brief window. To digital circuits and microcontrollers that operate at megahertz or gigahertz frequencies, each bounce looks like an independent button press.
- **Solution:** Engineers use **debouncing** techniques—either via hardware (e.g., RC filter circuits, Schmitt triggers, SR flip-flop latches) or software (e.g., polling after a short delay, state-filtering algorithms)—to ensure only a single clean transition is registered.

**Analysis of incorrect options:**
- **a) Buffering:** A technique where temporary memory storage (a buffer) is used to hold data while transferring it between devices or processes operating at different speeds or intervals.
- **c) Sampling:** The process of converting a continuous analog signal into a discrete signal by measuring its value at uniform, periodic time intervals.
- **d) Sharing:** A mechanism or design allowing multiple entities (processes, users, or hardware units) to utilize the same resource concurrently.
