# 2016S_FE_78

![[2016S_FE_AM_Q78_p1.png]]

a) 7,080
b) 7,600
c) 7,800
d) 8,000
?
b) 7,600

### Explanation

Under the **FIFO (First-In, First-Out)** method of inventory valuation, it is assumed that the oldest inventory items purchased are sold first. Consequently, the inventory remaining on hand at the end of the accounting period consists of the most recently acquired units.

### Step 1: Calculate Total Units and Ending Inventory Quantity

- **Beginning inventory (Jan 1, 2014)**: 100 units
- **Purchase #1**: 500 units
- **Purchase #2**: 400 units
- **Total goods available for sale**: $100 + 500 + 400 = 1,000 \text{ units}$
- **Total units sold**: 400 units
- **Ending inventory units**: $1,000 - 400 = 600 \text{ units}$

### Step 2: Allocate the 400 Units Sold (Earliest In)

Under FIFO, the 400 units sold are taken from the earliest available stock:
1. All 100 units from Beginning inventory (@ $10)
2. 300 units from Purchase #1 (@ $14)
   - Total units sold accounted for: $100 + 300 = 400 \text{ units}$

### Step 3: Value the Ending Inventory (600 Units)

The 600 units remaining in ending inventory come from the newest stock:
1. **Remaining from Purchase #1**: 
   $$500 - 300 = 200 \text{ units} \times \$14 = \$2,800$$
2. **All units from Purchase #2**: 
   $$400 \text{ units} \times \$12 = \$4,800$$

$$\text{Ending Inventory Value} = \$2,800 + \$4,800 = \$7,600$$

Thus, the ending inventory value at the end of the year 2014 is **b) 7,600**.
