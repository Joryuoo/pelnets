# 2016S_FE_77

![[2016S_FE_AM_Q77_full.png]]

a) 64.50
b) 65.00
c) 65.40
d) 70.00
?
b) 65.00

### Explanation

Under the **periodic average method** (also known as the **weighted-average cost method**), the unit cost of inventory is determined once at the end of the accounting period by dividing the total cost of goods available for sale by the total number of units available for sale.

$$\text{Unit Price} = \frac{\text{Total Cost of Beginning Inventory} + \text{Total Cost of Purchases}}{\text{Total Quantity of Beginning Inventory} + \text{Total Quantity of Purchases}}$$

### Step 1: Calculate Total Quantity and Total Cost of Goods Available for Sale

From the given table, sum the beginning inventory and all purchases received during the period:

| Date | Transaction | Received Quantity | Unit Price | Total Cost ($) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Carryover from previous month | 200 | 60 | $200 \times 60 = 12,000$ |
| 2 | Purchase | 500 | 70 | $500 \times 70 = 35,000$ |
| 10 | Purchase | 800 | 60 | $800 \times 60 = 48,000$ |
| 20 | Purchase | 500 | 70 | $500 \times 70 = 35,000$ |
| **Total** | | **2,000** | | **$130,000** |

### Step 2: Compute the Periodic Average Unit Price

$$\text{Unit Price} = \frac{\$130,000}{2,000 \text{ units}} = \$65.00$$

This unit price of **65.00** is applied to value both the inventory shipped during the month ($1,700 \text{ units} \times 65.00 = \$110,500$) and the carryover for next month ($300 \text{ units} \times 65.00 = \$19,500$).

Therefore, the correct unit price is **b) 65.00**.
