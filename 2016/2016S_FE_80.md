# 2016S_FE_80

Which of the following is a criminal act related to a computer virus according to the Convention on Cybercrime?

a) A person creates a new virus to develop and test antivirus software.
b) A person forwards a received e-mail that is infected with a virus without being aware of the infection.
c) A person who found a virus that was created by a stranger stores the virus for the purpose that he/she can later run it on another person’s computer.
d) A person who received and found a virus offers it to an antivirus organization with a clear indication of a virus’s presence.
?
c) A person who found a virus that was created by a stranger stores the virus for the purpose that he/she can later run it on another person’s computer.

### Explanation

The **Convention on Cybercrime** (often referred to as the **Budapest Convention**) is an international treaty aimed at harmonizing national criminal laws against cyber threats, establishing investigative procedures, and fostering international cooperation.

Under **Article 6 (Misuse of Devices)**:
- It is a criminal offense to intentionally produce, sell, procure for use, import, distribute, or **possess a computer program (malware/virus) designed or adapted primarily for the purpose of committing offenses**, with the intent that it be used to commit unauthorized access, system interference, or data damage.
- Storing or retaining malware with the specific intent to execute it on another person's computer system without authorization constitutes intentional, unlawful possession with intent to commit cyber offenses. Hence, **c)** is a criminal act.

### Analysis of Incorrect Options:
- **a)** Developing malware in an isolated, controlled research environment specifically for testing, studying, or building antivirus defenses does not involve intent to commit an unlawful offense.
- **b)** Inadvertently forwarding an infected email without knowing it contains malware lacks the required criminal intent (*mens rea*) to be deemed a crime.
- **d)** Submitting an identified malware specimen to an antivirus vendor or security research institute with clear labeling is a lawful, standard defense and vulnerability reporting practice.
