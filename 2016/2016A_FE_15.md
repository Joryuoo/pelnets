### Explanation
This problem models an online real-time transaction processing system using the **$M/M/1$ queuing model**:
- The arrival of transactions follows a **Poisson distribution** with average arrival rate $\lambda$.
- The processing time follows an **exponential distribution** with average service rate $\mu$ (mean service time $T_s = \frac{1}{\mu}$).
- The single processor (CPU) acts as the server, with CPU utilization defined as:
  $$\rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu} \quad (0 \le \rho < 1)$$

---

#### Response Time Formula in an $M/M/1$ System:
The average response time $T_r$ consists of the queuing time (waiting time) plus the service time:
$$T_r = \frac{T_s}{1 - \rho} = \frac{1}{\mu - \lambda}$$

#### Behavior of the Curve:
1. **At $\rho = 0$ (no workload):**
   $$T_r = \frac{T_s}{1 - 0} = T_s > 0$$
   The response time does not start at 0; it equals the transaction's pure processing time $T_s$.
2. **As $\rho \to 1$ (utilization approaches 100%):**
   The denominator $(1 - \rho)$ approaches $0$, causing $T_r$ to increase sharply and asymptotically approach infinity:
   $$\lim_{\rho \to 1^-} T_r = \infty$$

This exponential-like asymptotic increase means that as the CPU becomes heavily loaded, queue lengths grow drastically, resulting in severe response time degradation.

---

#### Evaluation of Graphs:
- **Graph a:** Correctly starts at a positive non-zero value ($T_s$) when utilization is 0, curves smoothly upward, and approaches infinity as CPU utilization approaches 1.
- **Graph b:** Shows a linear relationship, which fails to account for queuing congestion near saturation.
- **Graph c:** Shows a concave/logarithmic curve leveling off near 1, which is the opposite of actual queue behavior.
- **Graph d:** Shows a constant response time independent of utilization, which is physically impossible in a queuing system.

Therefore, **a)** is the correct graph.
