b) A: 1, B: 1, C: 0, D: 1

### Explanation

In UML class / entity relationship diagrams, multiplicity notation represents the allowed range of cardinalities (minimum and maximum participation):

$$\text{Customer } \xleftrightarrow{\text{ A..B }} \text{ Register } \xleftrightarrow{\text{ C..D }} \text{ Car}$$

- Multiplicity placed next to an entity indicates **how many instances of that entity can be associated with ONE instance of the opposite entity**.

---

#### 1. Determining $A..B$ (Multiplicity at the Customer side)
- The multiplicity $A..B$ next to **Customer** defines: *For a single Car instance, how many Customers can it be registered to?*
- Examining the instance mapping in the diagram:
  - Car `1456` $\rightarrow$ linked to **1** customer (Mr. A)
  - Car `4568` $\rightarrow$ linked to **1** customer (Ms. B)
  - Car `3246` $\rightarrow$ linked to **1** customer (Mr. D)
- Every car is associated with at least 1 customer (minimum $A = 1$) and at most 1 customer (maximum $B = 1$).
- Therefore:
  $$A = 1,\quad B = 1 \quad (1..1)$$

---

#### 2. Determining $C..D$ (Multiplicity at the Car side)
- The multiplicity $C..D$ next to **Car** defines: *For a single Customer instance, how many Cars can that customer be registered to?*
- Examining the instance mapping in the diagram:
  - Mr. A $\rightarrow$ registered to **1** car
  - Ms. B $\rightarrow$ registered to **1** car
  - Ms. C $\rightarrow$ registered to **0** cars (she has no link)
  - Mr. D $\rightarrow$ registered to **1** car
- The minimum participation is 0 (since Ms. C has no car, $C = 0$).
- The maximum participation shown is 1 (each customer has at most one car, $D = 1$).
- Therefore:
  $$C = 0,\quad D = 1 \quad (0..1)$$

---

#### Conclusion:
- $A = 1$
- $B = 1$
- $C = 0$
- $D = 1$

This matches row **b)**.
