### Explanation

A **Pareto chart** is one of the fundamental Quality Control (QC) tools used to prioritize problems and identify the "vital few" causes from the "trivial many" (based on the 80/20 Pareto principle).

A standard Pareto chart consists of two components:
1. **Bar Graph**:
   - Each bar represents the frequency, cost, or count of a particular category (such as defect types or failure causes).
   - The bars are strictly arranged in **descending order** of magnitude from left to right (the largest contributor is positioned on the far left).
2. **Cumulative Percentage Line (Ogive)**:
   - A line graph superimposed over the bars representing the running cumulative total (or cumulative percentage from 0% up to 100%).
   - Because the bars decrease in height from left to right, the cumulative curve rises monotonically with a diminishing slope (concave downward).

### Analysis of the Options:
- **b)** **Correct**: Bars are ordered in descending order from highest to lowest, and the line represents the rising cumulative curve.
- **a)** **Incorrect**: The bars are arranged in ascending order (lowest to highest), which reverses the Pareto rule.
- **c)** **Incorrect**: The line connects the tops of the individual bars as a declining frequency polygon rather than tracking the cumulative sum.
- **d)** **Incorrect**: The bars form a unimodal distribution with the peak in the center (similar to a bell-shaped histogram or normal distribution), not sorted in descending order.
