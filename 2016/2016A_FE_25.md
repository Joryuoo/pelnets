# 2016A_FE_25
Which of the following is an explanation of the morphing technique in computer animation?

a) A hand-drawn outline is read with a scanner, then each enclosed area is filled with a single color.
b) Based on the digital data collected from the actual movement of a body, multiple images with a realistic movement are created.
c) In order to make a perspective two-dimensional animation, each image of moving parts is superposed on the background one by one in descending order of its distance from the viewer.
d) In order to represent a smooth transition between two images, multiple intermediate images are created for interpolation.
?
d) In order to represent a smooth transition between two images, multiple intermediate images are created for interpolation.
### Explanation
**Morphing** (short for *metamorphosis*) is a special effect and computer animation technique:

- **Definition:** It produces a seamless, continuous transformation of one image or 3D object into another.
- **Process:**
  1. Key feature points (landmarks) on the starting image are paired with corresponding feature points on the target image.
  2. The computer applies mathematical **interpolation** (in-betweening/tweening) to gradually warp the geometry and cross-dissolve the colors over time.
  3. A sequence of intermediate frames is generated so that the transformation appears fluid, natural, and continuous.
- Therefore, option **d)** accurately defines the morphing technique.

**Analysis of incorrect options:**
- **a) Digital cel coloring / Flood fill:** In traditional-to-digital animation workflows, hand-drawn key frames are scanned, and line art boundaries are filled digitally using bucket/flood fill tools.
- **b) Motion capture (mocap):** A technology that records the real-life physical movements of live actors using optical, magnetic, or inertial sensors and transfers that movement data onto 3D digital character rigs.
- **c) Painter's algorithm / Multiplane layering:** A depth-sorting rendering technique where background layers and objects farther from the camera are drawn first, followed by nearer objects superposed on top, simulating 2D/2.5D visual depth.
