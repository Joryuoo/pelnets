# 2016A_FE_27
![[2016A_FE_AM_Q27_p1.png]]

a) A: 208, B: Credit
b) A: 208, B: Debit
c) A: 510, B: Credit
d) A: 510, B: Debit
?
d) A: 510, B: Debit
### Explanation
This question combines principles of double-entry bookkeeping and relational database data modeling.

#### 1. Accounting Analysis (Double-Entry Bookkeeping):
- The transaction states: *"the sales of 500 dollars to Company C are booked in the cash account on April 4, 2015"* (recorded under Transaction Number `0122`).
- Under double-entry bookkeeping rules:
  - **Cash** is an **Asset**. When cash is received, the asset increases, which is recorded as a **Debit**.
  - **Sales** is a **Revenue** account. When sales revenue is earned, revenue increases, which is recorded as a **Credit**.
- Therefore, the balanced journal entry is:
  - **Debit:** Cash 500
  - **Credit:** Sales 500

#### 2. Mapping to Database Tables:
From the provided tables:
- **`Account` Table:**
  - `208`: Sales
  - `510`: Cash
- **`Transfer` Table for Transaction Number `0122`:**
  - Row 2 records:
    - `Account Code`: `208` (Sales)
    - `Debit/Credit`: `Credit`
    - `Amount`: `500`
    - `Transaction Number`: `0122`
  - Row 1 records:
    - `Account Code`: `A`
    - `Debit/Credit`: `B`
    - `Amount`: `500`
    - `Transaction Number`: `0122`

#### 3. Determining A and B:
- The UML model specifies the constraint: *"The total 'Debit' amount of one (1) accounting transaction must match the total 'Credit' amount."*
- Since Row 2 already records the `Credit` of 500 to `Sales` (Code `208`), Row 1 must record the matching `Debit` of 500 to the `Cash` account.
- Looking up `Cash` in the `Account` table gives `Account Code = 510`.
- Therefore:
  - **A = 510**
  - **B = Debit**

This combination corresponds to option **d)**.
