# 2016S_FE_61

In the Information Technology Infrastructure Library (ITIL), lowering an indicator that represents maintainability is given as an example of a key performance indicator (KPI) for availability management. Which of the following corresponds to this indicator?

a) Mean time between failures
b) Mean time between service incidents
c) Mean time to restore services
d) The number of interruptions within a fixed period
?
c) Mean time to restore services

### Explanation

In ITIL Availability Management, performance and quality are evaluated through key availability and serviceability metrics:

- **Maintainability**: Measures how quickly and effectively a configuration item (CI) or IT service can be restored to normal working status after a failure.
  - **MTRS (Mean Time to Restore Service)** or **MTTR (Mean Time to Repair)** is the primary metric for maintainability.
  - A **lower MTRS** means shorter recovery times and less downtime, which directly improves maintainability and overall availability. Therefore, lowering MTRS is a standard KPI for maintainability.

### Analysis of Other Options:
- **a) Mean time between failures (MTBF)**: Measures the **reliability** of a system/component (the average operational uptime between consecutive failures). For reliability, the goal is to **increase** (maximize) MTBF, not lower it.
- **b) Mean time between service incidents (MTBSI)**: Measures the total elapsed time from when one system incident occurs to when the next occurs. Like MTBF, it is a measure of overall system **reliability and stability**, where higher values are desirable.
- **d) The number of interruptions within a fixed period**: While tracking the frequency of outages is a useful metric for overall service stability, it is an indicator of **frequency of failure** rather than the formal ITIL indicator for **maintainability**.
