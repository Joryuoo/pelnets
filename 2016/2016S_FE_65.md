# 2016S_FE_65

Which of the following is an appropriate sequence of procedure for creating a business model?

A. Associating a business process with an existing organization  
B. Clarifying a business process  
C. Associating a business process with an information system  
D. Clarifying a data class (information model)  
E. Associating a business process with a data class  

a) B → D → C → E → A
b) B → D → E → C → A
c) D → B → C → E → A
d) D → B → E → C → A
?
b) B → D → E → C → A

### Explanation

In enterprise architecture, information systems planning, and business modeling methodologies (such as IBM's Business System Planning - BSP), a structured top-down approach is used:

1. **Step B (Clarifying a business process)**: Identify and define the fundamental business processes and activities necessary to fulfill the organization's business objectives.
2. **Step D (Clarifying a data class / information model)**: Identify and define the major data classes and entities (e.g., Customer, Product, Order) that are created or utilized by the business.
3. **Step E (Associating a business process with a data class)**: Map processes to data classes (often using a CRUD matrix) to determine how data is created, read, updated, and deleted by each business process.
4. **Step C (Associating a business process with an information system)**: Based on the processes and their shared data needs, identify and define the information systems or application subsystems that support them.
5. **Step A (Associating a business process with an existing organization)**: Map the business processes to existing organizational units, departments, and roles. Organizational structures change more frequently than fundamental business processes, so this mapping is performed last to ensure the core model remains stable and adaptable.

Therefore, the logical progression is **B → D → E → C → A**, corresponding to **Option b**.
