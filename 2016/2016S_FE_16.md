### Explanation
- **Striping (RAID 0):** A technique where data is segmented into consecutive units (bits, bytes, or blocks) and distributed across multiple physical storage disks concurrently. In the figure, bits $b_0, b_1, b_2, b_3$ are distributed across Data disks 1 through 4, followed by $b_4, b_5, b_6, b_7$, and so forth on a bit-by-bit basis. By spreading I/O operations across multiple disks working in parallel, data transfer rates and throughput are substantially improved.
- **Blocking:** The aggregation of multiple logical records into a single physical block on secondary storage to reduce the number of physical I/O read/write operations.
- **Disk cache:** High-speed semiconductor memory located on a hard drive or storage controller used to temporarily store recently accessed or frequently used data to minimize access latency.
- **Mirroring (RAID 1):** Writing identical copies of data concurrently to two or more disks to provide high fault tolerance and data redundancy.
