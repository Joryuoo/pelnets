a) A
### Explanation
**ROI (Return on Investment)** measures the gain generated on an investment relative to the amount invested:

$$\text{ROI} = \frac{\text{Total Profit}}{\text{Amount of Investment}}$$

Since the discount rate / IRR baseline is zero, future cash flows are not discounted. Calculating the total profit and ROI for each opportunity over the 5-year span:

* **Opportunity A:**
  * Total Profit: $15 + 30 + 45 + 30 + 15 = 135$
  * Investment: $100$
  * $\text{ROI} = \frac{135}{100} = 1.35$ ($135\%$)

* **Opportunity B:**
  * Total Profit: $105 + 75 + 45 + 15 + 0 = 240$
  * Investment: $200$
  * $\text{ROI} = \frac{240}{200} = 1.20$ ($120\%$)

* **Opportunity C:**
  * Total Profit: $60 + 75 + 90 + 75 + 60 = 360$
  * Investment: $300$
  * $\text{ROI} = \frac{360}{300} = 1.20$ ($120\%$)

* **Opportunity D:**
  * Total Profit: $105 + 105 + 105 + 105 + 105 = 525$
  * Investment: $400$
  * $\text{ROI} = \frac{525}{400} = 1.3125$ ($131.25\%$)

Comparing the four opportunities:
$$\text{ROI}_A (1.35) > \text{ROI}_D (1.3125) > \text{ROI}_B (1.20) = \text{ROI}_C (1.20)$$

Therefore, **Opportunity A** yields the greatest effect on an ROI basis.
