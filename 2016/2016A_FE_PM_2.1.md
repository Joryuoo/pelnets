### Explanation
**Blank A:**
The question asks for the internal signed magnitude representation of (-27).
For a 9-bit representation:
- Sign bit: `1` (since it's a negative number).
- Magnitude bits: `27` in binary is `11011`. Since we need 8 magnitude bits, we pad it with leading zeros to get `00011011`.
Putting it together: `1` + `00011011` = `100011011`.
This matches option **c**.

**Blank B:**
The sign of the product in multiplication is negative if the signs of the operands differ, and positive if they are the same.
MS is the sign of the multiplicand. QS is the sign of the multiplier.
- `0` and `0` -> `0` (+)
- `0` and `1` -> `1` (-)
- `1` and `0` -> `1` (-)
- `1` and `1` -> `0` (+)
This truth table corresponds to the Exclusive-OR (XOR) logic, which is denoted by the symbol `^`.
Therefore, PS = `(MS ^ QS)`.
This matches option **c**.

**Blank C:**

Let's trace the execution for (-3) × (+9).
Initially, the multiplicand magnitude is `0011` (3). It's loaded into bit positions 1 through 4 of MM, so `MM = .00011000.`
The multiplier magnitude is `1001` (9). It's loaded into QM, so `QM = .1001.`

After step [5]: 
Test the rightmost bit of QM (`1`). Copy MM to PM.
`PM = .00011000.`

Iteration 1 of [6]:

- [6-2] Shift QM 1-bit right: `QM = .0100.`
