### Explanation
The goal of the loop control circuit is to output `Z = 1` ONLY when all inputs (O8, O4, O2, O1) are 0. For any other combination, it must output `Z = 0`. This describes a 4-input NOR gate.

The overall logic function we need to implement is: `Z = NOR(O8, O4, O2, O1) = NOT(O8 OR O4 OR O2 OR O1)`.
The circuit implements this using three 2-input logic gates:
`Z = E( D(O8, O4), D(O2, O1) )`

There are two valid configurations from the options given:

**Option 1 (b, e):**
If we use OR gates for `D` and a NOR gate for `E`:
- The top D gate outputs `O8 OR O4`
- The bottom D gate outputs `O2 OR O1`
- The E gate takes these two outputs and applies NOR: `NOT( (O8 OR O4) OR (O2 OR O1) )`
This is exactly equivalent to a 4-input NOR, perfectly satisfying the requirement. So `D = OR` (b) and `E = NOR` (e) is a correct pair.

**Option 2 (e, a):**
If we use NOR gates for `D` and an AND gate for `E`:
- The top D gate outputs `NOT(O8 OR O4)`
- The bottom D gate outputs `NOT(O2 OR O1)`
- The E gate takes these two and applies AND: `NOT(O8 OR O4) AND NOT(O2 OR O1)`
By De Morgan's Laws, `(NOT X) AND (NOT Y)` simplifies to `NOT(X OR Y)`.
Thus, it yields `NOT( (O8 OR O4) OR (O2 OR O1) )`, which is also a 4-input NOR. So `D = NOR` (e) and `E = AND` (a) is another valid combination.

Since both combinations achieve the correct logic, selecting either `b, e` or `e, a` correctly fills the blanks for D and E.
