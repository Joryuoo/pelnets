For **Blank A** and **Blank B** (Firewall A settings):
Firewall A connects the Internet and the DMZ. The Mail server "sends and receives e-mails to and from external entities." The second rule allows incoming mail to the Mail server on port 25. For the Mail server to *send* emails to external entities, it needs to initiate SMTP connections to the Internet. Therefore, the source must be the Mail server (Blank A) and the destination port must be 25 (Blank B). Thus, Blank A is `c` (Mail server) and Blank B is `b` (25).

For **Blank C** and **Blank D** (Firewall B settings):
Firewall B connects the DMZ and the corporate LAN. The management PC on the corporate LAN can "send and receive e-mails to and from external entities via Mail server." The settings already allow the Management PC to send emails to the Mail server on port 25 (rule 5). To *receive* emails from the Mail server, the Management PC needs to connect to the Mail server using a mail reception protocol like POP3 (port 110). Therefore, the destination must be the Mail server (Blank C) and the destination port must be 110 (Blank D). Thus, Blank C is `c` and Blank D is `d`.
