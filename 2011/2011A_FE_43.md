# 2011A_FE_43

A message with a digital signature is received via e-mail. Which of the following can be checked by verifying the digital signature of the received message?

a) The e-mail has not been leaked.
b) The e-mail has not been relayed improperly.
c) The message has not been falsified.
d) The message has not been retransmitted on a specific date and time.
?
c) The message has not been falsified.

### Explanation
### Explanation
A **digital signature** ensures two primary security properties for a message:
1. **Integrity**: Proof that the message has not been altered or falsified in transit.
2. **Non-repudiation and Authentication**: Proof of the sender's identity (the sender cannot deny sending it).

When the recipient verifies the digital signature using the sender's public key, it confirms that the hash of the received message matches the original hash created by the sender, thus proving the message's integrity.

**Why other options are wrong:**
- **a)**: Digital signatures do not encrypt the message content, so they cannot prevent or detect if the e-mail was leaked (read by unauthorized parties).
- **b)**: Relaying improperly relates to network routing, which is not verified by a digital signature.
- **d)**: Protection against replay attacks usually involves timestamps or nonces, not just a standard digital signature.
